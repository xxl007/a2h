# Cash vs points/credits/gift cards: what respondent and expert marketplaces have learned

Scope note: covers cash-paying platforms (Prolific, MTurk, Respondent, UserTesting, User Interviews, JustAnswer, GLG/AlphaSights/Guidepoint, Clarity.fm, Intro.co) against points/credit-paying ones (Swagbucks, Google Opinion Rewards, YouGov, InboxDollars, Branded Surveys, generic MR panels). Claims are dated; "vendor" marks self-reported or commercially interested sources. Research done 2026-10-21; roughly 25 web searches/fetches. Several primary pages (Cint incentive-control help page, ScienceDirect Swiss panel paper, Prolific researcher pay-floor page) returned 403/404 and are cited via abstracts or secondary summaries where noted.

## Q1. Is there published evidence on respondent preference for cash vs points/gift cards, and how it varies with payment size and participant income/skill?

### Takeaway
Behavioural evidence (randomised incentive experiments) consistently finds cash out-performs vouchers/gift cards on response rate, including a 2026 experiment where $5 cash beat a $5 Amazon e-gift card and a split $2+$3 cash design beat everything by ~10 points. Stated-preference surveys are mixed and mostly vendor-run: panel-research firms find "money" first (60%), gift-card vendors find gift cards first (54%). Evidence on how preference varies with pay size or income/skill is thin; the one relevant finding is that cash preference was higher in behaviour than in stated preference, and that higher pay reduces attrition on Prolific.

### Cited Findings
- Randomised 2x2 experiment in Wave 4 of a US registered-voter panel (NSSEHE, n≈1,327 invited, ~330/arm): end-of-field response rates were Amazon $5 → 68%; cash $5 → 69%; Amazon $2 + $3 follow-up → 68%; cash $2 + $3 follow-up → 79%. Cash out-performed Amazon e-gift cards; splitting the incentive helped only with cash. The cash $2/$3 arm had the lowest cost per complete (0.91 relative to $5 cash) and the lowest nonresponse bias. Authors suggest the gift-card offer was "less noticed" in the invitation. Published 2026, Survey Research Methods (Yan/NORC, Arrue & Cantor/Westat) — [Source](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/srm/article/download/8476/7942/37242?inline=1)
- Same study, stated preference (934 respondents): ~50% preferred an Amazon gift card, 24% a digital payment (Venmo/Zelle/PayPal/Apple-Google Pay), 8% physical gift/debit card, 7% mailed cash, 7% cheque, 3% other e-gift. Stated preference (gift card) diverged from behaviour (cash worked better). 2026 — [Source](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/srm/article/download/8476/7942/37242?inline=1)
- Long-term incentives experiment across three waves of a mature Swiss web panel (pen vs 10-franc cash card vs 10-franc banknote): the banknote produced the highest response; effects of the other incentives were "much lower and statistically insignificant"; authors note the subjectively evaluated value of an incentive can matter as much as face value. Social Science Research, 2019 (abstract only; full text 403) — [Source](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0049089X18305581)
- Stated preference, panel-research vendor (RMS, 2015, pre-2018 but the most-cited figure): 60% prefer money, Amazon gift cards a distant second at 25% as first choice — [Source](https://rmsresults.com/2015/05/19/most-preferred-survey-incentives-by-research-panel-participants/)
- Stated preference, gift-card vendor (Blackhawk Network, undated marketing page): 54% of research participants prefer prepaid or gift cards "more than any other type, including cash" (vendor; sells gift cards) — [Source](https://blackhawknetwork.com/solutions/rewards-incentives/customer-loyalty/market-research)
- Stated preference, gift-card vendor (Tango Card ebook, undated): most-chosen option was gift cards and prepaid Visa/Mastercard (vendor) — [Source](https://info.tangocard.com/hubfs/eBook_MaximizingResearchParticipationAndOptimizingIncentives_v2.pdf)
- Stated preference, MR agency survey (Drive Research, 13 May 2025, sample size not stated, 71% female): most appealing were sweepstakes for $100 Visa / Amazon / choice-of-brand cards; least appealing were charity donations and a guaranteed $2.50 gift card; Amazon preferred brand 80%; rankings "consistent across age, education and survey experience" (no per-group numbers). Cash as a category was not offered as an option — [Source](https://www.driveresearch.com/market-research-company-blog/market-research-incentives-new-survey-shows-what-reward-is-most-appealing-to-respondents/)
- Pay level vs quality: randomised study of 225 Prolific participants found higher pay reduced attrition and correlated with more accurate task performance; added instructions had no effect (Ritchey et al., PLOS ONE, 2023) — [Source](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10550147/)
- Contrary older finding (Crump et al. 2013, via a Columbia review): higher wages lowered dropout but did not raise answer quality; an MTurk-India study found pay near local minimum wage did affect quality — [Source](https://journals.library.columbia.edu/index.php/gsjp/article/download/14129/7944/42143)
- Prolific's researcher guidance (vendor) states higher pay is linked to better data and cites Ritchey — [Source](https://researcher-help.prolific.com/en/articles/445230-prolific-s-payment-principles)
- MTurk workers paid only in Amazon gift-card balance (seven interviews, Pakistan, Colombia, Philippines, Venezuela, Brazil, India, Kenya, April–May 2024): gift balances "cannot cover basic needs"; converting them loses "a significant share" of wages and is "almost a secondary job"; all wanted bank deposit as an option and argued choice would motivate "more and better work" (Data Workers' Inquiry, 2024) — [Source](https://data-workers.org/?p=508)

### Inferences
- When the instrument itself is money-like (PayPal, Venmo, bank), stated preference for "gift card" largely reflects convenience/instant delivery, not a desire for restricted value: in the 2026 SRM study 24% wanted a digital cash transfer, and only 7% wanted mailed cash, while cash still won behaviourally.
- Vendor surveys that find gift cards win are run by gift-card issuers and typically exclude "cash to bank/PayPal" as an option; treat the 54% figure as a floor on "wants something fast and digital", not evidence that restricted credit beats cash.
- No study found tests "platform credit redeemable later for cash" against immediate cash. The closest analogue (Amazon gift balance for MTurk international workers) was experienced as a wage cut.

### Gaps
- No published breakdown of cash-vs-points preference by income or skill level; physician and expert-network sources (Q5) only list payout methods, not preferences.
- No study on how preference changes with payment size in the €2–€60 range; the SRM study tested only $5 total.

## Q2. What breakage / unredeemed-points rates do panel and loyalty programmes report, and how do accountants treat them?

### Takeaway
The only hard panel-incentive breakage data found is Greenfield Online's SEC correspondence (2005–2007): forfeitures ran 7–13% of gross incentive expense in North America (where panelists were notified and given 30 days) and 25–48% in Europe (where they were not notified), with a six-month inactivity expiry. Loyalty-industry benchmarks are 20–35% (vendor), retail gift-card breakage ~3–6%. Under IFRS 15/ASC 606, expected breakage is recognised as revenue in proportion to redemption, which is what makes points financially attractive to the issuer, and it is exactly the behaviour participants complain about.

### Cited Findings
- Greenfield Online (online panel company) 10-K/SEC correspondence, 3 June 2008: incentives are forfeited when a panelist fails to start a survey within six months; North American panelists were notified and given ~30 days to reactivate or request payment, European panelists were not notified. Gross incentive expense / unclaimed (forfeited), $ thousands: North America 2005 $6,483 / $631; 2006 $4,067 / $507; 2007 $4,290 / $317. Europe 2005 $3,176 / $967; 2006 $5,048 / $2,405; 2007 $6,190 / $1,561. Consolidated 2007 $10,480 / $1,878. Europe accrued incentives net of an estimated forfeiture rate (historical average with six-month lag, adjusted quarterly); North America switched to the same net method 1 Jan 2008 — [Source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001108906/000095012308006525/filename1.htm)
- Greenfield Online, Feb 2006: reduced the North American incentive expiration threshold from one year to six months of inactivity (FY2006 10-K) — [Source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001108906/000095012307003912/y31088e10vk.htm)
- Accounting method (ASC 606 / IFRS 15, PwC Revenue guide, ch. 7.4): unexercised rights are "breakage"; an entity recognises estimated breakage as revenue in proportion to the pattern of rights exercised; if not expected, it is recognised when the likelihood of exercise becomes remote — [Source](https://viewpoint.pwc.com/dt/us/en/pwc/accounting_guides/revenue_from_contrac/revenue_from_contrac_US/chapter_7_options_to_US/74unexercised_rights_US.html)
- Milliman worked example: IFRS 15 / ASC 606 allocate transaction price on relative standalone selling price; liability = cost per point × outstanding points × (1 − ultimate breakage rate); their airline example assumes 90% ultimate redemption / 10% breakage, an entity-specific estimate — [Source](https://www.milliman.com/en/insight/loyalty-programs-the-crown-jewels)
- Loyalty-programme benchmarks (vendor): "most programs target 20–35% breakage", healthy range 20–30% (Exchange Solutions glossary) — [Source](https://exchangesolutions.com/glossary/breakage/); 26.2% of all points unspent across programmes; CPG retail 20–30%, B2B/travel 70–85% (Rivo, Feb 2026, marketing blog, no primary source) — [Source](https://www.rivo.io/blog/points-expiry-impact-statistics)
- Gift-card breakage: Mercator Advisory Group estimated ~3% of gift-card dollars go unredeemed (~$3bn of $98.6bn loaded in 2019); Bankrate survey put unused balances at ~$187/person, ~$23bn total; Starbucks reported $212m breakage revenue in 2022 (AP, Dec 2023) — [Source](https://www.news4jax.com/business/2023/12/25/the-secret-life-of-gift-cards-heres-what-happens-to-the-billions-that-go-unspent-each-year/)
- Gift-card breakage recognised by listed companies estimated at $4.22bn for reporting year 2017 (SSIR) — [Source](https://ssir.org/articles/entry/using_gift_card_breakage_to_democratize_philanthropy); retailer disclosures ~3% (Nordstrom), 4.7–5.7% (Guess), 2015-era 10-Ks via Audit Analytics — [Source](https://blog.auditanalytics.com/gift-cards-a-present-to-bottom-line/)
- Google Opinion Rewards: credits expire one year from the most recently completed survey (Google help page) — [Source](https://support.google.com/consumersurveys/answer/3424361?hl=en); in Nov 2019 Google began enforcing expiry and "wiped out any balance that outlasted its expiration", triggering user complaints (Android Police, 13 Nov 2019) — [Source](https://www.androidpolice.com/2019/11/13/google-opinion-rewards-credits-disappearing-update/); 9to5Google, 9 Dec 2019 — [Source](https://9to5google.com/2019/12/09/google-opinion-rewards-credits/)
- YouGov: points stored in a "YouGov Wallet" until balance is high enough to exchange (YouGov panel page) — [Source](https://yougov.com/about/panel); third-party reviews say accounts idle 12 months can lose points and points "have no cash value" (sidehusl, affiliate site) — [Source](https://sidehusl.com/yougov/)

### Inferences
- Computed from Greenfield's figures: forfeiture as a share of gross incentive expense was North America 9.7% (2005), 12.5% (2006), 7.4% (2007); Europe 30.4% (2005), 47.6% (2006), 25.2% (2007); consolidated 16.5% / 31.9% / 17.9%. The 3–4x gap between the regions is explained by the notification policy, not by panelists' behaviour: silent expiry roughly triples breakage.
- A credits system that (a) has a redemption threshold, (b) expires on inactivity and (c) does not notify, will predictably retain 25–50% of earned value; one that notifies and has a low threshold retains under 10%. If A2H wants credits for operational reasons but not to profit from breakage, it must publish and account for that explicitly, since IFRS 15 would otherwise push breakage into revenue.
- No modern panel company (Dynata, Cint, YouGov) publishes a breakage rate; the Greenfield disclosure exists only because the SEC asked.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve YouGov plc or Cint AB annual-report notes on panel incentive liability; Cint's "Revenue and incentive control" help page returned 403.
- No 2018+ primary panel-industry breakage figure; everything recent is loyalty/retail.
- How EU accountants treat breakage under IFRS 15 for a marketplace paying individuals (as opposed to customer loyalty) was not researched; the PwC/Milliman material is customer-option accounting.

## Q3. What minimum payout thresholds and cadences do platforms use, and what complaints do they generate?

### Takeaway
Cash platforms cluster at low thresholds and fixed cadences (Prolific £6/$6 once per day via PayPal/Hyperwallet with a 72-hour cool-off for new or changed accounts; JustAnswer, Intro.co $20; Clarity 14 days; UserTesting ~7–14 days after approval; User Interviews up to 10 business days). Points platforms range from $1–5 (Swagbucks gift cards), $2 (Google Opinion Rewards), $15–30 (InboxDollars), to ~5,000 points ≈ $50 (YouGov). The dominant complaints are: balance stalling just below the threshold, account deactivation with a balance inside, delayed gift-card delivery, and ineligibility of payout methods by country.

### Cited Findings
Cash platforms
- Prolific (participant help, fetched 2026-10-21): minimum £6.00/$6.00 approved balance; one cash-out per 24h (midnight UTC); 72-hour cool-off until four cash-outs have been made or whenever the PayPal address is changed; moved from PayPal-direct to Hyperwallet in 2024 and reset every account to the cool-off; unclaimed payments return to the Prolific balance after 30 days and block further cash-outs; PayPal name/address must match the Prolific account; VPN/iCloud Private Relay triggers country-mismatch errors — [Source](https://participant-help.prolific.com/en/articles/445064-i-m-having-issues-cashing-out)
- Prolific pay floor: third-party comparison states Prolific enforces at least £6/$8 per hour, with no requester-set floor on MTurk — [Source](https://casrai.org/compare/prolific-vs-mturk); a 2018 Prolific blog post set the floor at £5/hour (~$7.50) — [Source](https://blog.prolific.co/instant/); Prolific's own payment-principles page recommends £9/$12 per hour (listed in search; not fetched) — [Source](https://researcher-help.prolific.com/en/articles/445230-prolific-s-payment-principles)
- MTurk: payment can be redeemed as Amazon.com gift certificate ("the only payment option available to international workers, apart from India") or transferred to a US bank account; by May 2019 ~60% of workers were US, ~30% India (Wikipedia) — [Source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Mechanical_Turk); worker forum reports some non-US countries later gained bank withdrawal via Hyperwallet with unpublished eligibility — [Source](https://www.mturkcrowd.com/threads/international-bank-transfer-eligibility-speculation-and-theories.5169/)
- User Interviews: when the platform pays, it pays only digital gift cards via Tremendous; researchers may choose PayPal/direct deposit; researchers have up to 10 business days after the session to release payment; most are issued within a day or two (secondary summary of help page) — [Source](https://www.finder.com/make-money-online/how-to-earn-money-user-testing)
- UserTesting: PayPal, once a researcher approves the session; one source says 14 days after the test — [Source](https://help.usertesting.com/hc/articles/11880240002845)
- Respondent: incentive sent via Tremendous after the session is verified (third-party guide) — [Source](https://moneypantry.com/sites-like-usertesting/)
- Contentsquare and Hotjar Engage participant panels: PayPal via Tremendous, 3–5 working days after session review — [Source](https://help.hotjar.com/hc/en-us/articles/36820026245905-FAQs-for-Getting-Paid-as-an-Engage-Research-Participant)
- JustAnswer: experts paid monthly once balance exceeds $20 (sidehusl) — [Source](https://sidehusl.com/justanswer/); PayPal, Venmo or direct deposit, $20 minimum; commission 20% of customer payment for new experts rising to 50% with high ratings (websiterating, affiliate) — [Source](https://www.websiterating.com/best-side-hustles/paid-expert-answers/)
- Clarity.fm: 15% service fee; PayPal only; payments processed within 14 days of the call — [Source](https://clarity.fm/help/articles/34/how-do-i-get-paid); [Source](https://clarity.fm/help/articles/64/why-do-payments-take-too-long)
- Intro.co: Stripe or PayPal, $20 minimum payout, commission varies by how the booking was made (third-party; no official page found) — [Source](https://paidfromsurveys.com/best-sites-that-pay-via-stripe)
- GLG: "most payments to our experts are processed within 7–10 business days after a completed consultation" (GLG reply on Trustpilot); reviewers report 5 business days, a 30-day wait, and trouble with a prepaid-card payout method — [Source](https://at.trustpilot.com/review/glginsights.com?page=7)
- AlphaSights: "standard terms of payment are 30 days, but we always aim to compensate as quickly as possible"; paid hourly pro-rata — [Source](https://www.alphasights.com/experts/)

Points / credit platforms
- Google Opinion Rewards: $2 minimum; Android users receive Google Play credit only, iOS users can cash out to PayPal; balances not transferable between platforms (Wikipedia) — [Source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Opinion_Rewards); expiry one year after the last completed survey — [Source](https://support.google.com/consumersurveys/answer/3424361?hl=en)
- Swagbucks: sources conflict ($1 gift cards / $5 PayPal; another gives £5 gift cards / £25 PayPal) — [Source](https://joingerald.com/learn/work--income/rewards-app-reviews-2026); analysis of 2026 reviews found 179 describing a payout that never completed, 73 of which say the account was shut with a balance inside; account deactivation is the most common complaint — [Source](https://unstar.app/blog/does-swagbucks-actually-pay-account-deactivated-cash-out-reviews-2026); cross-app review: "earnings crawl toward a payout threshold and then stall, with survey availability drying up exactly as the balance gets close" — [Source](https://unstar.app/blog/swagbucks-survey-junkie-inboxdollars-branded-surveys-prolific-make-money-apps-ranked-2026)
- InboxDollars: $30 minimum in most sources; the platform's own materials conflict ($30 vs $15 first request then $10); $3 processing fee on cheque cash-outs (2026 review) — [Source](https://pixelscan.net/blog/inboxdollars-review-2026-is-it-worth-your-time/); $30 vs Swagbucks $5 (2022) — [Source](https://www.savvynewcanadians.com/inboxdollars-review/)
- YouGov: ~5,000 points ≈ $50 / £50 threshold; Trustpilot ~4.5 stars from 100,000+ reviews; common complaint is the slow climb to threshold, plus individual reports of $50 gift cards not arriving for weeks — [Source](https://www.thepennyhoarder.com/make-money/yougov-review/); [Source](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/yougov.com?page=2)

### Inferences
- The cash platforms' "complaint surface" is identity/KYC friction (Prolific name/address match, country mismatch, Hyperwallet migration reset), not value loss. The points platforms' complaint surface is value loss (thresholds, expiry, deactivation with balance). A2H's Friday batch plus lagging ID verification already sits on the cash-platform side of that line; adding a credits layer would import the second complaint class without removing the first.
- Threshold height correlates with reputation damage: YouGov ($50) and InboxDollars ($30) draw "slow to reach" complaints; Prolific ($6) and Google ($2) draw few threshold complaints.

### Gaps
- Could not find current official Swagbucks, Branded Surveys or Toluna threshold pages; figures above are from affiliate/review sites and conflict.
- No Reddit r/beermoney threads surfaced directly (search tooling returned summaries only).
- Guidepoint payment terms not found.

## Q4. Did any platform switch from points to cash or the reverse, and what happened?

### Takeaway
No documented case of a panel switching from points to cash (or back) with published outcome data was found. The documented changes are narrower: MTurk extending bank payouts to some non-US workers who were previously gift-card-only (unofficial), Prolific moving PayPal payouts to Hyperwallet in 2024 (added KYC friction), User Interviews consolidating on Tremendous (for country coverage and W-9 collection), Google enforcing credit expiry in 2019 (backlash), and Greenfield cutting expiry from 12 to 6 months in 2006 (to raise breakage).

### Cited Findings
- Prolific moved payouts to Hyperwallet in 2024 and reset all accounts to the 72-hour cool-off, requiring four new requests before daily cash-outs resume — [Source](https://participant-help.prolific.com/en/articles/445064-i-m-having-issues-cashing-out)
- User Interviews switched incentive vendor to Tremendous because the previous vendor could not pay some countries (e.g., South Africa) and did not collect W-9s, forcing manual Amazon payments and manual tax collection; migration took two engineers two weeks; bulk sending saved ~8 hours/month; the company says "up to 40% of companies experience research incentive fraud" (vendor case study, undated) — [Source](https://www.tremendous.com/case-studies/user-interviews-switch-to-tremendous)
- MTurk: gift certificates were the only option for non-US, non-India workers (Wikipedia); forum reports of Hyperwallet bank withdrawals for some countries with unpublished eligibility — [Source](https://www.mturkcrowd.com/threads/international-bank-transfer-eligibility-speculation-and-theories.5169/); 2024 worker interviews show gift-card-only payment persisted for workers in seven countries — [Source](https://data-workers.org/?p=508)
- Google Opinion Rewards began enforcing one-year expiry in Nov 2019 and wiped balances over a year old (Android Police) — [Source](https://www.androidpolice.com/2019/11/13/google-opinion-rewards-credits-disappearing-update/)
- Greenfield Online cut incentive expiry from one year to six months of inactivity in Feb 2006 and later moved to net-of-forfeiture accrual — [Source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001108906/000095012307003912/y31088e10vk.htm)

### Inferences
- The direction of every documented change is either toward more cash/choice (MTurk, User Interviews) or toward more breakage (Google, Greenfield); the former are framed as fixing complaints, the latter generate them.

### Gaps
- No platform found that publicly moved from cash to points, or published signup/retention/quality deltas after a payment-type change.

## Q5. For expert networks and micro-consulting: how are experts paid, how fast, and what matters to them?

### Takeaway
Every high-skill marketplace found pays cash (bank, PayPal, Hyperwallet, cheque), at hourly or per-answer rates of roughly $75–$500/hour, with net 7–30 day terms; none pays points. Physician panels that offer gift cards offer them as one option alongside PayPal/ACH/cheque, with explicit fee/speed trade-offs. What experts complain about is speed and reliability of payment and opaque commission cuts (JustAnswer), not payment form.

### Cited Findings
- AlphaSights: hourly rate, pro-rata, net 30 — [Source](https://www.alphasights.com/experts/)
- GLG: claims >$1bn in consulting fees paid to Network Members since 1999 — [Source](https://glg.com/ja/how-we-help/expert-network); 7–10 business days after consultation (GLG Trustpilot reply) — [Source](https://at.trustpilot.com/review/glginsights.com?page=7); forum anecdote: ~$200/hour, user quit after a year — [Source](https://www.early-retirement.org/threads/paid-research.125895/page-2); third-party guide: experts often charge $250–$500 per hour-call; one expert reports >$35,000 earned with no prep or follow-up work (affiliate site) — [Source](https://expertopportunities.com/expert-networks/)
- Arbolus expert FAQ on what expert networks pay (vendor) — [Source](https://arbolus.com/resources/how-much-do-expert-networks-pay)
- Physician panels (Sermo): direct deposit, PayPal, gift cards or charity donation (White Coat Investor, affiliate) — [Source](https://www.whitecoatinvestor.com/surveys-for-money-best-companies-for-physician-online-surveys/); fee table: Amazon gift card free, 1–5 days; cheque $5, 3–14 days; PayPal via Hyperwallet $0.25, 1–5 days (Physician on Fire, affiliate) — [Source](https://www.physicianonfire.com/is-sermo-legit/); ZoomRx pays "immediately" via Zelle/PayPal/ACH; M3 Global pays by cheque/PayPal/gift card, $20–$150 per survey (SalaryDr table, 2026, low-authority) — [Source](https://www.salarydr.com/blog/best-paid-medical-surveys-physicians-2026)
- JustAnswer experts (Indeed reviews): paid per question, sometimes one payment split across several questions; customer disputes can cut pay; declining low-pay terms can drop the rate; one expert earns ~$1,000/month as a side job — [Source](https://www.indeed.com/cmp/Just-Answer); April 2024 ex-expert removed after ~10 years without warning, automated support — [Source](https://www.pissedconsumer.com/people/22696581.html)
- Clarity.fm: 15% fee, PayPal only, 14-day processing — [Source](https://clarity.fm/help/articles/34/how-do-i-get-paid)
- Tax handling is a procurement criterion for the platform side: User Interviews chose Tremendous partly for W-9 collection — [Source](https://www.tremendous.com/case-studies/user-interviews-switch-to-tremendous)

### Inferences
- Experts are already in a world of net-30 invoicing and 1099/self-assessment; a Friday batch is faster than GLG/AlphaSights norms and would not be a differentiator problem. A credits ledger that defers the cash a further cycle would be slower than any expert network found.
- No expert platform found that would ask a doctor or lawyer to accumulate "credits"; the gift-card option in physician panels exists to avoid payout fees, not because physicians prefer restricted value.
- For EU experts, the key tax question is the platform's reporting duty (DAC7-type seller reporting) and whether earnings are self-employed income; whether value is held as credits or cash does not change taxability once earned, but it may change the timing of when the expert is deemed to have received it. Not sourced here; flag for a tax adviser.

### Gaps
- No physician- or lawyer-forum thread found that directly discusses accepting points instead of cash; absence of such offers is itself the signal.
- Hacker News threads on GLG/expert networks did not surface via the search tool.
- Guidepoint terms and JustAnswer's official expert payment page were not retrieved.

## Q6. Does paying in platform credit lower quality or attract fraud farms vs cash?

### Takeaway
There is no controlled study isolating payment form (credit vs cash) from platform vetting. What exists: (1) platform comparisons where the cash platform with strict ID verification (Prolific) produced far higher quality than MTurk (cash/gift-card, weak vetting); (2) industry claims that any reward attached to an open link, "such as a gift card", attracts bots and farms; (3) a 2023 JMIR trial where 75.6% of early enrolees were bots until a CAPTCHA was added. Fraud follows liquidity plus weak identity, not points per se; gift cards are attractive to farms because they are liquid and resellable.

### Cited Findings
- Douglas, Ewell & Brauer, PLOS ONE, 14 Mar 2023: all crowd platforms paid $0.96 for a ~7.5-minute study ($7.50/h). High-quality respondent rate: Prolific 67.9%, CloudResearch 62.0%, Qualtrics 53.2%, SONA 52.8%, MTurk 26.4%; cost per high-quality respondent Prolific $1.90, CloudResearch $2.00, MTurk $4.36, Qualtrics $8.17. Unique geolocation: MTurk 53.4% vs Prolific 89.9%. Authors did not test pay or payment form as a cause; they attribute CloudResearch's gain to filters (approved list, duplicate-IP and suspicious-geolocation blocks, country verification) — [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10013894/)
- Peer et al., Behavior Research Methods, 2022: only Prolific gave high data quality on all measures without filters; MTurk showed "alarmingly low" quality even with filters; lowest quality came from MTurk workers reporting the site as their main income but working few hours — [Source](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/data-quality-of-platforms-and-panels-for-online-behavioral-resear/)
- Prolific safeguards (vendor): closed pool; email + phone verification; Onfido face scan; IP detection; account freeze if verification not completed; continuous monitoring of submission patterns, logins and device signals; separates fraudulent accounts (addressed by ID verification) from scripted bots and "agentic AI" — [Source](https://researcher-help.prolific.com/en/articles/445227-how-does-prolific-prevent-duplicate-participant-accounts); [Source](https://researcher-help.prolific.com/en/articles/621821-methodological-justification-pack-how-prolific-protects-data-quality)
- JMIR 2023 remote-trial recruitment: of 1,142 enrolees in the first two months, 75.6% (863) were bots; none after a CAPTCHA was added; a further 5% were false participants — [Source](https://www.jmir.org/2023//e46523/)
- Industry claims (vendors): fraud in panel-sourced surveys "widely reported at 30 to 50 percent", some say 70% (Accelerant Research talk, Dec 2024) — [Source](https://www.accelerantresearch.com/arvic/december-2024-arvic/talk-9_gmWpT4wUA); Realeyes: >40% of 200,000+ sessions from three large panels were low quality — [Source](https://blog.realeyesit.com/billion-dollar-decisions-at-risk-the-alarming-rise-of-survey-fraud); Pollfish distinguishes bots from "survey farms" (networks of real people with multiple panelist accounts) — [Source](https://ws.pollfish.com/resources/blog/market-research/survey-data-quality/); Latana: open-link surveys with a reward "such as a gift card" are the most exposed; attention checks alone catch 5–10%; switching incentives to charity donations or promo codes reduces appeal to farms — [Source](https://www.latana.com/article/how-to-avoid-survey-fraud-for-higher-quality-survey-data)
- MTurk workers paid in gift-card balance describe converting it as a loss-making "secondary job" (2024) — [Source](https://data-workers.org/?p=508); the Tremendous case study says User Interviews bulk-blocked its top offending IPs and was testing vendor fraud tooling — [Source](https://www.tremendous.com/case-studies/user-interviews-switch-to-tremendous)

### Inferences
- The quality gap between Prolific and MTurk is attributed by the authors to vetting and closed pools, not to payment form; both are cash (MTurk also gift card). So the evidence supports "verify identity before value becomes liquid", not "pay in credits".
- Credits redeemable for cash are just deferred cash: they do not deter farms unless redemption is gated on verification, in which case the gate, not the credit, does the work. A2H's lagging ID verification "until payout" already provides that gate on a cash wallet.
- The farm-deterrent form of incentive in the industry literature is non-liquid (charity, promo codes), which is incompatible with paying experts €2–€60.

### Gaps
- No study found that randomises payment form (cash vs gift card vs credit) and measures fraud or answer quality; all fraud-rate figures are vendor-reported.
- No Prolific-published fraud or removal rate for 2024–2025.

## Q7. What transfers to A2H (EU, €2–€60 per answer, reserve-plus-bid auction, Friday Stripe Connect batches, ID verification lagging until payout)?

### Takeaway
The evidence favours keeping a cash wallet with a low threshold, a published cadence, and verification gating the first payout; a credits layer buys A2H little that Stripe Connect plus a pending balance does not, and imports the breakage and threshold-stall complaints that define the points platforms.

### Cited Findings
- Prolific's model of an "approved balance", £6/$6 minimum, daily cadence, cool-off for new/changed accounts, 30-day unclaimed-payment return — [Source](https://participant-help.prolific.com/en/articles/445064-i-m-having-issues-cashing-out)
- Cash with a small immediate portion plus a conditional second portion ($2 + $3) out-performed lump cash and all gift-card arms — [Source](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/srm/article/download/8476/7942/37242?inline=1)
- Silent expiry tripled breakage vs notified expiry (Greenfield, 2005–2007) — [Source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001108906/000095012308006525/filename1.htm)
- Expert networks pay cash at net 7–30 days — [Source](https://www.alphasights.com/experts/); [Source](https://at.trustpilot.com/review/glginsights.com?page=7)

### Inferences
- "Credits earned by answering, redeemable for cash" is functionally identical to Prolific's "approved balance" if (a) 1 credit = €1 fixed, (b) no expiry, (c) a low threshold (≤ €6–10), (d) redemption is automatic on Fridays once verified. If any of those differ, A2H is building a points programme, and the complaint and breakage patterns of Q2–Q3 apply. Naming it "balance" rather than "credits" avoids signalling restricted value to experts, who have never been offered points elsewhere.
- Experts (first market) will benchmark against GLG/AlphaSights (net 7–30, cash): a Friday batch beats both; a credits step that delays further would be the slowest in the category. The public (second market) will benchmark against Prolific (£6, daily): a Friday batch with a ≤ €10 threshold is competitive; a €25–50 threshold is YouGov/InboxDollars territory.
- The reserve-plus-bid auction already lets A2H split pay (reserve now, bid uplift on acceptance); the SRM result suggests a visible "immediate + conditional" cash structure may raise participation more than any change of instrument.
- If A2H's motive for credits is accounting (holding float, avoiding micro-payout fees), the research says to solve that with batching and thresholds on a cash wallet; if the motive is breakage revenue, expect 7–13% with notification and 25–48% without, and expect it to be the top complaint theme.
- Fraud: the Prolific/MTurk gap suggests identity-gated redemption is the control that matters; A2H's "verify before first payout" on a cash wallet delivers that without a separate currency.

### Gaps
- No source on Stripe Connect payout-fee economics vs float income for a credits ledger; not researched.
- EU-specific tax/e-money implications of holding user "credits" redeemable for cash (possible e-money/stored-value regulation) were out of scope and not sourced; flag for legal review.
