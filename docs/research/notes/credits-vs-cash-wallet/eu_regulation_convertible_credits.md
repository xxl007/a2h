# EU/French regulatory, tax and accounting treatment of cash-convertible platform credits (as of 21 Oct 2026)

Scope note: this covers an EU marketplace (likely a French SAS) where business "principals" buy credits to pay individual "participants" for answers, and participants can convert earned credits to euros. Throughout, findings are tagged **[CLEARLY REQUIRED]**, **[DEPENDS ON STRUCTURE]** or **[UNKNOWN / NOT FOUND]**. Several primary texts (EUR-Lex pages for DAC7 and the voucher directive, the ACPR position PDF, Légifrance article pages) could not be fetched directly and are reported via secondary sources or the UK legislation.gov.uk mirror; this is flagged where relevant.

## Key question 1: When does a redeemable platform credit become e-money or a payment service (EMD2 / PSD2 / PSD3-PSR), and how do the limited-network and commercial-agent exclusions apply to a two-sided marketplace?

### Takeaway
A credit that is bought for cash, stored electronically, and can be spent to pay a third party (the participant) and then redeemed for cash fits the EMD2 definition of e-money; the two exclusions a marketplace could invoke are both narrow and both being tightened under PSD3/PSR. The clean way to stay outside the perimeter is for a licensed PSP (Stripe) to hold the funds and for the "credit" to be a ledger/unit-of-account overlay, not a stored monetary value issued by the platform. PSD3/PSR are agreed but still not in the Official Journal as of October 2026.

### Cited Findings

**EMD2 / PSD2 framework (law in force in 2026)**
- EMD2 (Directive 2009/110/EC) defines electronic money as electronically stored monetary value, issued on receipt of funds for the purpose of making payment transactions, and accepted by persons other than the issuer; a reward balance that is free to earn and only usable with the issuer tends to fail the "issued in exchange for consideration" element. **[CLEARLY REQUIRED test]** — [DNB: What is an electronic money institution](https://www.dnb.nl/en/sector-information/supervision-sectors/electronic-money-institutions/authorisation-as-an-electronic-money-institution-overview/what-is-an-electronic-money-institution/); [prodata.de practitioner note on loyalty points](https://www.prodata.de/kundenbindung/en/?p=13945)
- Issuing e-money without authorisation or exemption is an offence; DNB states it can impose cease-and-desist orders and administrative fines and refer to prosecutors (economic offence). The French equivalent is agrément by the ACPR under the Code monétaire et financier (CMF). — [DNB: prohibition on operating as an EMI without authorisation](https://www.dnb.nl/en/sector-information/open-book-supervision/open-book-supervision-sectors/electronic-money-institutions/authorisation-as-an-electronic-money-institution-overview/prohibition-on-operating-as-an-electronic-money-institution-without-authorisation/)
- CJEU, C-661/22 *ABC Projektai* (22 Feb 2024): a licensed payment institution receiving and holding funds on a payment account for future payment transactions, even without a specific payment order and for more than 48 hours, does not by itself constitute issuance of e-money. Firm commentary adds the court looked at whether a "separate asset" is created and accepted by the customer and kept separate from the funds transferred; Plesner warns supervisors can still treat a product indistinguishable from an e-money wallet as circumvention. — [Motieka](https://motieka.com/news/victory-at-the-court-of-justice-of-the-eu-setting-a-significant-precedent-for-financial-institutions/); [Plesner](https://plesner.com/insights/articles/2024/02/payment-institutions-as-wallet-providers?sc_lang=en); [Vixio](https://www.vixio.com/insights/pc-top-eu-court-decision-re-aligns-payments-and-e-money-requirements)

**Limited network exclusion (PSD2 Art. 3(k); French CMF L.525-5 / L.521-3-1)**
- EBA Guidelines EBA/GL/2022/02 (final report 24 Feb 2022, applying from 1 June 2022) set the test. Guideline 2.1 requires for a "limited network": (a) a direct contractual agreement between the issuer and each provider of goods/services and each acceptor; (b) an envisaged maximum number of providers set out in the notification; (c) goods/services offered under a common brand that characterises the network. Guideline 4.1–4.2 requires, for "limited range of goods or services", a functional connection via a specific category with a common purpose identified by the issuer. — [EBA/GL/2022/02 final report (mirror PDF)](https://www.finanssivalvonta.fi/contentassets/fe9063c85d0a4a48bd8a155055c99c43/2022_2_en_final-report.pdf); [EBA press release](https://www.eba.europa.eu/eba-publishes-final-guidelines-limited-network-exclusion-under-payment-services-directive)
- Guideline 1.3: "in the cases where funds are transferred to the payment instrument by using an intermediary other than the issuer, the transfer of funds should be considered as a separate payment service that does not fall within the scope of the service excluded under Article 3(k)". Guideline 1.9: competent authorities "should not take into account the redeemability of the monetary value stored on the payment instrument" when assessing Art. 3(k) — i.e. being refundable neither helps nor hurts the exclusion. Guideline 6.2: notification is due as soon as the value of transactions exceeds EUR 1 million in any period shorter than 12 months; Guideline 6.1: notification in each Member State where users are located and the threshold is exceeded. — [EBA/GL/2022/02](https://www.finanssivalvonta.fi/contentassets/fe9063c85d0a4a48bd8a155055c99c43/2022_2_en_final-report.pdf)
- On marketplaces, the EBA's feedback table says: online marketplaces "could potentially benefit from the LNE, provided that they meet the requirements of Article 3(k)... and the provisions of these Guidelines", but "online marketplaces tend to continuously grow their acceptance network" so competent authorities "should... treat these business models with caution due to the possibility for some of the specific instruments to develop into general-purpose instruments", citing PSD2 Recital 14 that instruments usable "in stores of listed merchants should not be excluded"; the EBA declined to add marketplace-specific criteria. **[DEPENDS ON STRUCTURE]** — [EBA/GL/2022/02, feedback table items 7–8](https://www.finanssivalvonta.fi/contentassets/fe9063c85d0a4a48bd8a155055c99c43/2022_2_en_final-report.pdf)
- France: CMF Art. L.525-5 lets a firm issue/manage e-money without agrément if used only on its premises, within a limited network of acceptors under a commercial agreement, or for a limited range of goods/services; the three criteria are alternatives; only firms whose e-money in circulation exceeds EUR 1 million must file a declaration with the ACPR. ACPR Position 2022-P-01 (replacing 2017-P-01, amended July 2023) transposes the EBA indicators and also covers the cash-loading derogation of R.561-16-1. — [ACPR instruction 2013-I-13 (Nov 2024 version)](https://acpr.banque-france.fr/sites/default/files/media/2024/11/20/20241120_instruction_2013-i-13.pdf); [ACPR Position 2022-P-01 (URL as indexed; PDF returned 404 when fetched)](https://cclrf.banque-france.fr/sites/default/files/media/2023/07/26/20230726_position_2022-p-01.pdf)

**Commercial agent exclusion (PSD2 Art. 3(b); French CMF L.521-3)**
- PSD2 Art. 3(b) excludes payment transactions "through a commercial agent authorised via an agreement to negotiate or conclude the sale or purchase of products or services on behalf of only the payer or only the payee". EBA Q&A 2020_5355 (final answer 6 Dec 2021, prepared by the Commission): a B2C platform "could be excluded from the scope of PSD2 if, as a commercial agent, it is authorised by the payee to negotiate or conclude the sale of goods or services and if it does not also act on behalf of the payer"; "The Competent Authority must evaluate, based on the business model, whether the platform only acts on behalf of the payee or for both payer and payee"; "PSD2 does not provide criteria in order to determine whether a platform can be deemed to be also acting on behalf of the payer." The PSD2 impact assessment said the exemption was meant for legal persons using an agent and "should not be used by agents working on behalf of consumers". **[DEPENDS ON STRUCTURE]** — [EBA Q&A 2020_5355](https://eba.europa.eu/single-rule-book-qa/qna/view/publicId/2020_5355); [EBA Q&A 2020_5354](https://www.eba.europa.eu/single-rule-book-qa/qna/view/publicId/2020_5354)
- The ACPR is reported to have stated that the commercial-agent exemption "ne s'applique pas aux places de marché telles qu'elles fonctionnent actuellement et qui relèvent donc du champ d'application de la régulation sur les services de paiement" (source page returned 403 on fetch; quote taken from search index of the article — verify). — [Actu-Juridique: Précisions de l'ACPR à propos de situations entraînant des exemptions d'agrément](https://www.actu-juridique.fr/affaires/bancaire-credit/precisions-de-lacpr-a-propos-de-situations-entrainant-des-exemptions-dagrement/)
- Stripe itself tells French Connect platforms they "must use account tokens" to reduce the risk of being seen as providing payment services or acting as an intermediary (Stripe FAQ, see Q2). — [Stripe Connect & PSD2 FAQ](https://stripe.com/sv-fi/guides/frequently-asked-questions-about-stripe-connect-and-psd2)

### Inferences
- A platform-issued credit that (i) is bought with cash, (ii) represents a claim against the platform, (iii) is used to pay participants (persons other than the issuer) and (iv) is redeemable for cash is e-money in substance; cash-redeemability is irrelevant to the LNE test (GL 1.9) but is exactly what makes the instrument look general-purpose rather than a closed-loop voucher.
- The LNE is a poor fit: the only "acceptors" would be an open, growing pool of individual participants, which is the pattern the EBA says CAs should treat with caution; the "limited range" limb (answers to questions) is arguable but the platform would still have to notify at EUR 1m and defend it.
- The commercial-agent exclusion is weak for a two-sided marketplace (platform acts for both sides) and the ACPR has reportedly said it does not apply to marketplaces as they operate today; PSR narrows it further.
- The practical consequence: either obtain an EMI/PI agrément (heavy) or keep all monetary value with a licensed PSP and make the "credit" a non-monetary unit of account/pricing abstraction, which is the structure analysed in Q2.

## Key question 2: Does using Stripe (Connect, separate charges and transfers, customer balance) keep the platform outside e-money rules, and what does Stripe allow regarding stored value, prepaid balances and credits?

### Takeaway
Stripe's own PSD2 FAQ says Connect platforms do not need a licence because funds are "never in the possession or control of the platform" and are held in Stripe-controlled safeguarded accounts; but Stripe's Restricted Businesses list (updated 22 Sep 2026) restricts "sale of stored value or credits maintained, accepted and issued by anyone other than the seller" and says "seller-maintained stored value or credits may be subject to limits" — so a platform-issued cash-redeemable credit is at best a restricted use case needing Stripe's approval, and Stripe Billing "credits" are a usage-pricing feature, not a cash-out wallet.

### Cited Findings
- Stripe Technology Europe, Limited (STEL) is authorised as an electronic money institution by the Central Bank of Ireland and passports across the EEA; the European Connected Account Agreement is issued by Stripe Payments Europe, Limited and incorporates the Stripe Services Agreement. — [Stripe Connect & PSD2 FAQ](https://stripe.com/sv-fi/guides/frequently-asked-questions-about-stripe-connect-and-psd2); [Stripe Connected Account Agreement](https://stripe.com/en-ch/legal/connect-account); [Stripe STEL authorised services](https://stripe.com/fr/legal/stel)
- Stripe: platforms need a PSD2 licence/exemption or agent appointment "only if they provide regulated payment services to their users"; Connect users "do not need a licence" because they do not hold or control user funds; end-customer payments "are never in the platform's possession or control"; funds sit in Stripe-controlled protected accounts to which the platform "has no access or management rights"; with separate charges and transfers the platform "provides pre-agreed instructions to Stripe" and "has no discretionary authority over the funds"; the platform "must be contractually authorised by the connected account" to send such instructions; French Connect platforms "must use account tokens" to reduce the risk of being seen as providing payment services or acting as an intermediary; Stripe does not consider its Connect platforms to be intermediaries because Stripe has a direct relationship with connected accounts. Stripe also flags that PSD3 is likely to narrow "both the commercial agent and limited network exemptions". **[DEPENDS ON STRUCTURE — these are Stripe's conditions]** — [Stripe Connect & PSD2 FAQ](https://stripe.com/sv-fi/guides/frequently-asked-questions-about-stripe-connect-and-psd2)
- Stripe Restricted Businesses (page "Last updated: 2026-09-22"), category "Non-fiat currency and stored value" (Restricted): "Sale of stored value or credits maintained, accepted and issued by anyone other than the seller"; "Seller-maintained stored value or credits may be subject to limits"; also restricted: "Pre-loaded payment cards, gift cards, virtual credits or other products and services in which a monetary value is stored within the item (digital or physical)". Prohibited: "Peer-to-peer money transmission"; Restricted: "Money transmitters, remittances... other money service businesses", "Escrow services", "Payment facilitation and aggregation (including receiving settlement proceeds for goods or services that you did not provide)". **[CLEARLY REQUIRED to check with Stripe]** — [Stripe Restricted Businesses (en-gb)](https://stripe.com/en-gb/legal/restricted-businesses)
- Stripe's blog explains "stored value or gift cards" are listed because they attract money-laundering and fraud risk. — [Stripe blog: why some businesses aren't allowed](https://stripe.com/blog/why-some-businesses-arent-allowed)
- Stripe Billing "credits": a Credit Grant "tracks a set of prepaid or promotional billing credits allocated to a customer"; grants apply only to subscription items linked to a metered price, after discounts and before taxes; there is an optional "fund the credit grant" step; no documentation found on cash-out or transfer of such credits to connected accounts. — [Stripe docs: billing credits](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/usage-based/billing-credits); [implementation guide](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/usage-based/billing-credits/implementation-guide)
- Stripe Financial Accounts for platforms (Treasury) has its own restricted list and requires explicit approval for some categories. — [Stripe Treasury requirements](https://docs.stripe.com/treasury/requirements)
- EBA Guideline 1.3 (above): funds loaded onto an instrument via an intermediary other than the issuer are a separate payment service, so Stripe acquiring the top-up does not itself exclude the platform's instrument. — [EBA/GL/2022/02](https://www.finanssivalvonta.fi/contentassets/fe9063c85d0a4a48bd8a155055c99c43/2022_2_en_final-report.pdf)

### Inferences
- Structure A (plain Stripe): principal pays by card/SEPA into Stripe; funds settle to Stripe's safeguarded account; platform uses separate charges & transfers to pay participants' connected (Express/Custom) accounts; Stripe pays out. The platform never holds value, so no EMI/PI licence is needed on Stripe's reading. A "credit" here can only be a display/pricing unit mapped 1:1 to euros already collected by Stripe or to a pre-funded Stripe customer balance.
- Structure B (platform-issued credits with its own redemption promise to participants, cash-out on request): the platform is the obligor on a monetary claim, which is e-money/payment-account-like regardless of Stripe being the rails; this also sits in Stripe's "stored value or credits" restricted category and arguably "payment facilitation... for goods or services that you did not provide".
- A hybrid where principals prepay into a Stripe customer balance (Stripe holds the funds) and the platform only draws down on answer acceptance keeps prepayment inside the EMI; the risk point is the participant side: any platform-held "earned balance" awaiting cash-out is a debt of the platform unless it lives as a Stripe transfer to the participant's connected account at the moment of earning.

### Gaps
- Stripe's "seller-maintained stored value or credits may be subject to limits" is not elaborated publicly; limits and approval process are unknown and must be asked of Stripe.
- No public Stripe guidance found on holding a long-lived, cash-redeemable participant balance at platform level under Connect.

## Key question 3: VAT — how does the voucher directive (2016/1065) treat purchased credits, when is VAT due, and how is the platform fee taxed as a spread versus an explicit commission?

### Takeaway
Credits are a "voucher" only if there is an obligation to accept them as consideration for goods or services with the supply or supplier identified; the directive expressly covers only instruments redeemable against goods/services, so a cash-redeemable credit is closer to a payment instrument than a voucher. If the credits are vouchers, they are almost certainly multi-purpose (supplier/place of supply unknown at issue), so VAT is due at redemption, not at purchase, while any identifiable platform service (distribution/promotion/intermediation) is taxed separately whether the fee is explicit or a spread.

### Cited Findings
- Directive 2016/1065 (adopted 27 June 2016) inserted Arts 30a, 30b, 73a into Directive 2006/112/EC, applying from 1 Jan 2019 to vouchers issued after 31 Dec 2018 (Art. 410a). — [Council Directive 2016/1065 text (legislation.gov.uk mirror)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2016/1065/data.htm?view=plain); [EUR-Lex record](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2016/1065/oj)
- Art. 30a(1): a voucher is "an instrument where there is an obligation to accept it as consideration or part consideration" for goods/services and "where the goods or services to be supplied or the identities of their potential suppliers are either indicated" on the instrument or related documentation. Art. 30a(2): single-purpose voucher (SPV) = "the place of supply... and the VAT due on those goods or services, are known at the time of issue". Art. 30a(3): MPV = "a voucher, other than a single-purpose voucher". — [2016/1065 text](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2016/1065/data.htm?view=plain)
- Art. 30b(1): each transfer of an SPV by a taxable person acting in his own name "shall be regarded as a supply of the goods or services to which the voucher relates" (VAT at issue/sale). Art. 30b(2): for an MPV, the actual provision of services "shall be subject to VAT pursuant to Article 2, whereas each preceding transfer of that multi-purpose voucher shall not be subject to VAT"; and where the transfer is made by a taxable person other than the supplier, "any supply of services that can be identified, such as distribution or promotion services, shall be subject to VAT". Art. 73a: taxable amount for an MPV supply = consideration paid for the voucher (or face value) less VAT. — [2016/1065 text](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2016/1065/data.htm?view=plain)
- Recital 4: "Only vouchers which can be used for redemption against goods or services should be targeted by these rules." Recital 12: the directive "does not target the situations where a multi-purpose voucher is not redeemed by the final consumer" and the consideration is kept by the seller; the Commission was to report on non-redeemed vouchers by end-2022. — [2016/1065 text](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2016/1065/data.htm?view=plain); [KPMG VAT newsletter July 2016](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/pdf/2016/07/vat-newsletter-july-2016-kpmg-en.pdf)
- CJEU C-637/20 *DSAB Destination Stockholm* (first ruling on the directive): the term "voucher" is broad; an instrument need not have a fixed face value, set duration or precisely defined range of goods/services to be a voucher; a city card giving access to varied services was an MPV. — [Baker McKenzie summary](https://insightplus.bakermckenzie.com/bm/tax/european-union-european-court-judgment-regarding-vat-treatment-of-vouchers)
- CJEU C-68/23 *M-GbR v Finanzamt O* (18 Apr 2024): prepaid codes to load a user account in an online shop can be SPVs if the place of supply and VAT are known at issue (e.g. codes restricted to residents of one Member State), even if traded through intermediaries in other Member States; a voucher is MPV only if it is not an SPV; distribution/promotion services by intermediaries are separately taxable. Commentators say the ruling leaves room for divergent national application. — [Meijburg](https://meijburg.com/news/cjeu-rules-vat-treatment-vouchers); [PwC Germany](https://blogs.pwc.de/de/german-tax-and-legal-news/article/242966/ecj-vat-treatment-of-prepaid-cards-or-voucher-codes-for-purchase-of-digital-content); [ITR](https://internationaltaxreview.com/article/2d562gj0lzwqomjqp9qm8/sponsored/cjeu-clarifies-what-a-multipurpose-voucher-is-anything-but-a-single-purpose-one); [BAILII text](https://www.bailii.org/eu/cases/EUECJ/2024/C6823.html)

### Inferences
- If the credit is redeemable for cash as well as for answers, it fails Recital 4's "redemption against goods or services" framing and looks like a payment instrument/prepayment rather than a voucher; VAT then follows the general rules on payments on account (VAT on a supply is chargeable when the payment on account is received if the supply is identified — Art. 65 VAT Directive) and on the platform's own service.
- If treated as a voucher: participants are unidentified at issue and may be in different Member States (place of supply unknown), so credits are MPVs; VAT on the answer (if any — individual participants are typically outside VAT or under the French franchise en base) arises at redemption; the platform's margin is taxable as a distribution/intermediation service either way.
- A spread model (buy credits at 1.00, pay participants 0.80) does not avoid VAT on the platform fee: Art. 30b(2) taxes "any supply of services that can be identified"; the spread is that consideration. An explicit commission is simply easier to invoice and to substantiate.
- Breakage (unredeemed MPV value) is outside the directive's scope (Recital 12): whether it is outside VAT depends on national practice; French position not found.

## Key question 4: Consumer and contract law — credit expiry, forfeiture and refund (EU/French), and B2B customers

### Takeaway
If the credit is e-money, French law (CMF Arts L.133-29 to L.133-38, transposing EMD2 Art. 11) gives the holder a right to redemption at par, on demand, free of charge, with only narrow fee exceptions and no contractual derogation against consumers; a B2B principal can be made to waive those rights by contract. For non-e-money closed-loop credits there is no specific French statute on expiry found; 12-month expiry clauses are common in gift cards and have been litigated under unfair-terms rules in the prepaid-telecom context.

### Cited Findings
- CMF L.133-29: e-money units are refunded by the issuer to the holder on request. L.133-30 (in force since 30 Jan 2013, Law 2013-100 of 28 Jan 2013): the refund is made "sans frais pour le détenteur". L.133-31: if the contract has a term, refund fees are possible only in three cases (request before the term; holder terminates before the term; request more than one year and one day after the term). L.133-35: a request within one year after the term or on termination triggers full refund. L.133-36: transfer costs for cash refunds fall on the issuer notwithstanding any clause. L.133-37: Arts L.133-30 to L.133-36 can be derogated by contract "sauf lorsque le détenteur est une personne physique agissant à des fins non professionnelles". L.133-38: issuer remains liable for refund when it uses a distributor. L.315-7: the contract must state refund conditions, deadline and any exceptional fees. **[CLEARLY REQUIRED if e-money]** — [Légifrance CMF Section 12 (L.133-29 à L.133-38)](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/section_lc/LEGITEXT000006072026/LEGISCTA000027005748/); [Art. ### Inferences
- Principals are businesses: if credits are e-money, the platform can contractually limit refund rights (L.133-37) but cannot escape the licensing consequence; if credits are mere prepayments under a B2B services contract, expiry/forfeiture clauses are governed by general contract law (Code civil) and the "déséquilibre significatif" rule of Code de commerce L.442-1 (not researched here).
- Participants are individuals (consumers for these purposes unless acting as professionals): any platform-held earned balance that is e-money carries an unwaivable on-demand, free redemption right; expiry/forfeiture of earned, cash-convertible balances would be the highest-risk consumer-law feature of a credit system.
- A plain Stripe Connect payout model has no "expiry" problem at all: once transferred, the money is the participant's in a Stripe account.

### Gaps
- No French statute or DGCCRF position specifically fixing gift-card/credit validity periods was found; forfeiture of B2C prepaid credits appears governed by unfair-terms law (Code de la consommation L.212-1) and case law, not retrieved in full.
- B2B unfair-terms analysis (Code de commerce L.442-1) not researched.

## Key question 5: Accounting — IFRS 15 / French GAAP treatment of unredeemed credits (breakage), deferred revenue, and whether participant-earned credits create a liability

### Takeaway
Under IFRS, cash-redeemable balances are financial liabilities (IAS 32 / IFRS 9), not IFRS 15 contract liabilities, so no breakage revenue can be recognised while the customer can still demand cash; only non-refundable prepayments for the platform's own services are contract liabilities with IFRS 15 B44–B47 breakage. Under French GAAP, prepaid amounts sit in compte 487 (produits constatés d'avance) or as dettes; no ANC guidance on breakage was found.

### Cited Findings
- IFRS 15 breakage guidance (B44–B47) applies to all customer contracts: if the entity expects to be entitled to a breakage amount, it recognises it "in proportion to the pattern of rights exercised by the customer"; otherwise "when the likelihood of the customer exercising its remaining rights becomes remote". — [Deloitte IAS Plus IFRS 15 summary](https://iasplus.com/content/104bf54d-e723-4435-a4be-a1a91ee591dd); [Deloitte Roadmap ASC 606 8.8 (US mirror)](https://dart.deloitte.com/USDART/home/codification/revenue/asc606-10/roadmap-revenue-recognition/chapter-8-step-5-determine-when/8-8-customers-unexercised-rights-breakage)
- IFRS Interpretations Committee (Sept 2015 tentative decision; Jan 2016 comment letters; continued March 2016): a prepaid card that is non-refundable to the holder but redeemable at third-party merchants gives the issuer "a contractual obligation to deliver cash" (to the merchants) with no unconditional right to avoid it, so it is a financial liability in scope of IAS 32/IFRS 9; breakage follows derecognition rules, not IFRS 15; members noted different answers where the card is redeemable with the issuer only (IFRS 15 interaction). — [IAS Plus Sept 2015](https://www.iasplus.com/en/meeting-notes/ifrs-ic/2015/september/ias-32); [IAS Plus Jan 2016](https://www.iasplus.com/en/meeting-notes/ifrs-ic/2016/january/ias-32); [IFRS staff paper AP5 Jan 2016](https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/meetings/2016/january/ifrs-ic/ias-32-classification-of-liability/ap5-prepaid-card-and-comment-letters.pdf)
- IFRS staff paper notes "recognition of unexercised customer rights ('breakage') differs" between financial and non-financial liabilities; for non-financial liabilities breakage goes to P&L when outflow is no longer probable (IAS 37). — [IAS Plus item](https://www.iasplus.com/content/8c791c07-1d6c-41ce-9745-d160cbb64ebf)
- US GAAP contrast: FASB ASU 2016-04 treats prepaid stored-value product liabilities as financial liabilities but permits breakage recognition by analogy. — [Deloitte EITF Snapshot Nov 2015](https://www.iasplus.com/en/publications/us/eitf-snapshot/2015/nov)
- Loyalty points conferring a material right are a separate performance obligation; revenue is deferred until redemption or expiry; expected redemption rate feeds the allocation. — [RSM UK](https://www.rsmuk.com/insights/advisory/accounting-for-customer-loyalty-schemes)
- French GAAP: compte 487 "Produits constatés d'avance" is used when an amount received relates to a later period; compte 4871 "Produits constatés d'avance sur jetons émis" is the PCG sub-account for goods/services to be delivered against issued tokens, including refund of unused tokens where commercial policy allows. Practitioner guidance on expired chèques-cadeaux (from the buyer's side) reclassifies to produits exceptionnels (771). — [Dougs compte 487](https://www.dougs.fr/ressources/pcg/classe-4/compte-487/); [Dougs compte 4871](https://www.dougs.fr/ressources/pcg/classe-4/compte-4871/); [Companeo](https://www.companeo.com/cheques-cadeaux/FAQ/quel-traitement-comptable-pour-les-cheques-cadeaux-non-distribues)
- UK: FRS 102 aligns with IFRS 15 revenue model from 1 Jan 2026. — [RSM UK](https://www.rsmuk.com/insights/advisory/accounting-for-customer-loyalty-schemes)

### Inferences
- Principal-purchased credits that are refundable in cash: financial liability at face value; no breakage income unless/until the refund right lapses (contractually or by prescription). Non-refundable credits usable only for platform services: contract liability with IFRS 15 breakage.
- Participant-earned, cash-convertible credits: a financial liability of the platform (obligation to deliver cash) from the moment they are credited; this is a balance-sheet item that does not exist under a Stripe Connect transfer model (the liability sits with Stripe/the participant's account).
- Credits that principals buy to pay participants are, economically, funds held for third parties; if the platform also recognises them as its own deferred revenue it will overstate revenue — only the platform's fee/spread is its revenue (agent presentation, IFRS 15 B34–B38 principal/agent, not researched here).

### Gaps
- Final IFRIC March 2016 agenda decision text not retrieved (page fetch returned empty); conclusion reported from the Sept 2015 tentative decision and Jan 2016 notes.
- No ANC (Autorité des normes comptables) regulation or French doctrine on breakage of prepaid credits found.
- IFRS 15 principal-vs-agent analysis for the marketplace fee not researched.

## Key question 6: Tax reporting — DAC7 obligations for platforms paying individuals; French participants (micro-entrepreneur/income tax); does paying in credits defer or change the reporting trigger?

### Takeaway
DAC7 (Directive 2021/514, in force from 1 Jan 2023; France: CGI Arts 1649 ter A–E, first reports by 31 Jan 2024) covers "personal services" by individuals with no de minimis threshold (the 30-transactions/EUR 2,000 exclusion is for goods only), and "consideration" is defined as compensation "paid or credited" to the seller net of platform fees — so crediting a participant's balance is already a reportable event; converting to euros later does not defer it. For participants, French micro-BNC/BIC taxation is on amounts received, and a platform social-contribution withholding pilot is slated from Oct 2026/Jan 2027.

### Cited Findings
- DAC7 is Council Directive (EU) 2021/514 of 22 March 2021, amending Directive 2011/16/EU. — [CMS France](https://cms.law/en/fra/news-information/dac-7-le-renforcement-de-la-cooperation-fiscale-pour-les-plateformes-numeriques)
- "Consideration" = "a payment in any form paid or credited to a seller for a relevant activity from which all fees, commissions or taxes withheld or charged by [the platform] have been deducted"; the platform must know or be able to reasonably know the amount; no consideration, no relevant activity. **[CLEARLY REQUIRED]** — [Belastingdienst DAC7 guidance](https://belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/en/business/content/information-for-platform-operators-dac7); [Vistra](https://www.vistra.com/insights/dac7-businesses-must-report-and-how-comply)
- Excluded sellers for goods: fewer than 30 relevant activities and total consideration not exceeding EUR 2,000 in the period; this exclusion does not extend to services. — [Belastingdienst](https://delta.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/en/business/content/eu-directive-administrative-cooperation-digital-platforms-dac7); [Irish S.I. 705/2022](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2022/si/705/made/en/pdf); [jechange.fr](https://www.jechange.fr/banques/news/vinted-leboncoin-30-ventes-plateforme-transmet-fisc-3-cas-imposables)
- France: transposed by Art. 134 of the 2021 Finance Act into CGI Arts 1649 ter A to 1649 ter E, with Decree 2022-1661; replaces the Art. 242 bis regime; applies to transactions from 1 Jan 2023 with the first declaration due before 31 Jan 2024; covers sale of goods, "provision of services by individuals", rental of transport and real estate; BOFiP commentary BOI-INT-AEA-30; model annual certificates for sellers are on impots.gouv.fr. — [Baker McKenzie France DAC7](https://insightplus.bakermckenzie.com/bm/tax/france-electronic-platforms-the-dac-7-directive-is-transposed-with-an-effective-date-of-1-january-2024-for-transactions-carried-out-in-2023); [Deloitte Société d'Avocats](https://blog.avocats.deloitte.fr/dac-7-nouvelles-precisions-administratives); [KPMG Avocats](https://kpmg.com/av/fr/avocats/eclairages/2023/02/preparation-des-operateurs-de-plateforme-a-dac-7.html)
- Platform sends data by end-January/February of the following year and gives each seller a summary of what was sent (identity, number of transactions, amounts collected, fees retained). — [jechange.fr](https://www.jechange.fr/banques/news/vinted-leboncoin-30-ventes-plateforme-transmet-fisc-3-cas-imposables)
- French micro-entrepreneur regime: flat-rate abatement on chiffre d'affaires HT; bookkeeping is a "livre des recettes" recording encaissements chronologically (cash basis). — [CCI Lyon Métropole](https://www.lyon-metropole.cci.fr/actualite/micro-entrepreneurs-renforcement-des-obligations-sur-les-plateformes-numeriques)
- Proposed platform withholding of micro-entrepreneurs' social contributions: platform "retient les cotisations sur chaque paiement et les reverse elle-même à l'Urssaf"; pilot with volunteer platforms from 1 Oct 2026, generalisation planned 1 Jan 2027; text "pas encore voté" per the source. **[PENDING]** — [jechange.fr](https://www.jechange.fr/banques/news/micro-entrepreneurs-plateformes-precompte-cotisations-2027-200-millions); [Assemblée nationale amendment](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/17/amendements/0324A/AN/3274.pdf)

### Inferences
- Because DAC7 counts consideration "paid or credited", a credit system does not defer the platform's reporting obligation; the reportable amount is the euro value credited net of fees, and the platform must be able to value credits in euros (which it can, if 1 credit = fixed euro amount). If credits had a floating or non-cash value, the "known or reasonably knowable" test could be harder to meet — a complication, not a relief.
- For the participant's own income tax (cash-basis micro regime), receipt of a cash-convertible credit is plausibly "encaissement" when it becomes freely convertible; no French authority found either way — treat as unknown and avoid designing around a deferral.
- A credits model with no de minimis (services) means every participant with any credited consideration is reportable, same as under direct Stripe payouts; DAC7 is neutral between the two models except for valuation complexity.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch the EUR-Lex text of Annex V; definitions taken from national tax-authority guidance and practitioner sources.
- No BOFiP/ministerial answer found on the income-tax timing of remuneration paid in convertible credits/points.
- Current BOFiP wording of BOI-INT-AEA-30 and 2025 Finance Act amendments to 1649 ter A not verified.

## Key question 7: AML/KYC — does cash-redeemable credit change KYC obligations versus direct Stripe Connect payouts?

### Takeaway
Yes: cash-redeemability removes the only AML simplification available to e-money (the low-value, non-reloadable, no-cash-out exemption), and if the platform issues e-money it becomes an obliged entity itself under French LCB-FT rules and, from 10 July 2027, the EU AMLR. Under Stripe Connect, Stripe performs the regulated KYC on connected accounts; the platform's own obligations are contractual/operational rather than those of an obliged entity.

### Cited Findings
- Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 (AMLR) applies from 10 July 2027 (football agents/clubs from 10 July 2029); until then national AMLD transpositions apply. — [Didit AMLR checklist](https://didit.me/de/blog/amlr-checklist-by-obliged-entity/)
- AMLR Art. 19(7): supervisors may exempt obliged entities from certain CDD measures for e-money only where (a) "the payment instrument is not reloadable, and the amount stored electronically does not exceed EUR 150"; (b) it "is used exclusively to purchase goods or services provided by the issuer"; (c) it "is not linked to a payment account" and "does not permit any stored amount to be exchanged for cash or for crypto-assets"; (d) the issuer monitors transactions. General CDD trigger: occasional transactions of at least EUR 10,000; fund transfers by credit/financial institutions at EUR 1,000. **[CLEARLY REQUIRED from July 2027]** — [AMLR Art. 19 (Springlex rendering of OJ L 2024/1624)](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/aml/amlr-regulation/article-19/)
- France (current): CMF R.561-16-1 allows deferred/simplified identity verification for e-money subject to caps; secondary sources cite verification required if loading/payments exceed EUR 150 in 30 days, cumulative loading above EUR 1,000, remote purchases above EUR 50, with cash top-ups barred outside limited networks and cash redemptions above EUR 100 subject to vigilance (figures from 2016–2018 commentary; verify on Légifrance). — [Weblex](https://www.weblex.fr/weblex-actualite/monnaies-electroniques-souplesse-dans-la-verification-d-identite-des-clients); [Option Finance on Decree of 10 Nov 2016](https://www.optionfinance.fr/entreprise-expertise/le-regime-juridique-de-la-monnaie-electronique-modifie-par-le-decret-du-10-novembre-2016-relatif-au-financement-du-terrorisme.html); [Légifrance JORF extract](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/article_jo/JORFARTI000036819691)
- ACPR Position 2022-P-01 also addresses the derogation to the cash-loading ban (R.561-16-1) for limited-network instruments. — [ACPR instruction 2013-I-13](https://acpr.banque-france.fr/sites/default/files/media/2024/11/20/20241120_instruction_2013-i-13.pdf)
- Industry letter (2023): the AMLD5 low-risk e-money exemption is "strictly limited to the purchase of goods and services in a single Member State". — [EMA joint call to action](https://e-ma.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Joint-industry-Call-to-Action-on-CDD-exemption.pdf)
- Stripe: STEL is the authorised EMI; Stripe has a direct relationship with connected accounts; under the recipient agreement the recipient has a relationship only with the platform. — [Stripe Connect & PSD2 FAQ](https://stripe.com/sv-fi/guides/frequently-asked-questions-about-stripe-connect-and-psd2); [Stripe Connected Account Agreement](https://stripe.com/GB/connect-account/legal)

### Inferences
- A cash-redeemable credit fails AMLR Art. 19(7)(b) and (c), so no simplified due diligence; if the platform is the issuer it must run full CDD on every holder (both principals and participants), transaction monitoring, and suspicious-activity reporting to TRACFIN — a full obliged-entity programme.
- Under Stripe Connect with Express/Custom accounts, the KYC burden (identity, bank account, sanctions) is Stripe's regulated obligation; the platform still needs fraud controls, but is not itself an LCB-FT obliged entity by virtue of payments.
- Cash-out of earned credits is the AML-sensitive leg (value in from businesses, value out to many individuals); a credit system therefore invites more scrutiny than a model where each payout is an attributable Stripe transfer for an identified answer.

### Gaps
- Current consolidated text of CMF R.561-16-1 not retrieved; thresholds quoted are from dated secondary sources.
- Whether a platform acting as Stripe "recipient"/Custom-account platform has any own AML registration duty in France was not confirmed.

## Key question 8: Enforcement cases or guidance on marketplace credits, gig-platform wallets or survey-panel points (EU/UK)

### Takeaway
No EU/UK enforcement case specifically about marketplace credits, gig wallets or survey-panel points was found; the closest items are a 2020 Liechtenstein FMA order against a platform issuing e-money without a licence, standing DNB/FCA warnings, and non-EU guidance (Singapore MAS, UAE) that points schemes escape regulation only when they are loyalty-type (free, closed-loop, not marketed as payment).

### Cited Findings
- FMA Liechtenstein (2020): prohibited a platform operator (ETR AG) from issuing e-money or accepting deposits/repayable funds and ordered repayment of funds received. — [FMA notice ETR AG](https://www.fma-li.li/en/warning/notice-etr-ag-209)
- DNB: providing e-money services without authorisation/exemption triggers enforcement (cease and desist, fines, criminal referral). — [DNB](https://www.dnb.nl/en/sector-information/open-book-supervision/open-book-supervision-sectors/electronic-money-institutions/authorisation-as-an-electronic-money-institution-overview/prohibition-on-operating-as-an-electronic-money-institution-without-authorisation/)
- Cambodia NBC (2026): businesses issuing unlicensed e-wallet credits and stored-value membership cards given 90 days to notify, with legal action threatened — a non-EU parallel. — [Cambodianess](https://cambodianess.com/article/central-bank-warns-of-action-against-unauthorized-e-wallets); [Tilleke](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/cambodia-increases-oversight-of-e-wallet-customer-programs/2/)
- MAS (Singapore) on loyalty points: looks at whether the programme is marketed as a loyalty programme or as a payment service and whether it promotes purchases from the issuer or specified merchants. — [MAS ask.gov.sg](https://ask.gov.sg/mas/questions/clx8ktis400bzryozdeghrztg)
- UAE Central Bank rulebook exempts reward schemes where tokens are issued against money paid by the issuer/merchant and usable only for the issuer's or merchant's goods/services. — [CBUAE rulebook](https://rulebook.centralbank.ae/node/5741)
- FCA PERG 3A: shopping-centre card excluded only with direct commercial agreements and no facility to spend outside. — [FCA PERG 3A.5](https://www.handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/PERG/3A/5.html)
- EBA Q&A 2020_5354 describes platforms that "collect the payments before the products/services are delivered and transfer the aggregate amounts to the payee on a regular basis" and invoke the commercial agent exclusion by making the payment "settlement of the payer's debt to the payee" — the EBA/Commission treat this as a CA-by-CA assessment. — [EBA Q&A 2020_5354](https://www.eba.europa.eu/single-rule-book-qa/qna/view/publicId/2020_5354)

### Inferences
- Absence of a published case does not indicate tolerance: the ACPR/DNB positions and the EBA's caution about marketplaces show supervisors expect marketplace-issued stored value to be licensed or to sit with a licensed PSP.
- Survey-panel and gig platforms that pay out via PSPs (bank transfer, PayPal, gift cards from third-party issuers) avoid issuing their own stored value; points that are cash-redeemable on demand are the feature that distinguishes them from loyalty schemes.

### Gaps
- No ACPR sanction or FCA enforcement notice about platform credits/wallets found for 2022–2026.
- No regulator guidance located on survey-panel points specifically (Prolific, Swagbucks, etc.).

## Key question 9 (synthesis): Is a credit system legally lighter or heavier than a prepaid cash wallet plus Stripe Connect payouts?

### Takeaway
Heavier, on every axis examined, unless the "credit" is purely a unit of account over money that Stripe holds and moves. A platform-issued, cash-convertible credit is e-money (licensing, refund-at-par rights, full AML programme, financial-liability accounting), sits in Stripe's restricted "stored value or credits" category, gives no VAT advantage (MPV at best, spread taxed anyway), and gives no DAC7 deferral ("paid or credited").

### Cited Findings
- Supporting findings are those cited in Q1–Q8 above; no additional sources.

### Inferences
- **[CLEARLY REQUIRED]** If the platform is the obligor on redeemable value: ACPR agrément as EME/EP or an agent arrangement; CMF L.133-29 ff. refund rights for participants; obliged-entity AML; financial-liability accounting; Stripe approval for stored value.
- **[DEPENDS ON STRUCTURE]** Whether LNE/commercial-agent exclusions could apply (unlikely for a two-sided, growing, cash-out marketplace; and narrowing under PSR); VAT voucher classification (MPV if voucher at all); whether principal prepayments can be non-refundable (B2B contract) to avoid financial-liability treatment.
- **[UNKNOWN]** French income-tax timing for participants paid in convertible credits; Stripe's internal limits on "seller-maintained credits"; final PSR article wording and application date (18 vs 21 months).
- Lightest compliant design: principals prepay into Stripe (customer balance or charges), participants are Stripe Express/Custom connected accounts, each accepted answer triggers a Stripe transfer; "credits" exist only as a pricing/display label with a fixed euro value and no independent redemption promise by the platform, and no platform-held earned balance.

### Gaps
- No law-firm briefing was found that analyses exactly this "earn credits, cash out" pattern for an EU marketplace; the conclusions above are inferred from the general rules and Stripe's published terms.
