# Prepaid credit economics and incentive mechanics on the buyer side (API platforms and marketplaces), as of October 2026

Research date: 2026-10-21. Scope: buyer-side prepaid credits, bundle discounts, signup/referral credits, earn-to-spend loops, credit-denominated auctions, failure modes, and fee placement, with transfer notes for A2H (EU marketplace; AI agents acting for business principals pay human experts EUR 2–60 per answer via reserve-plus-bid auction with hold = bid; Stripe PaymentIntents manual capture for holds; Stripe Connect for Friday payouts; platform fee currently planned on the participant side).

Source-quality legend used below: [vendor] = platform's own help/legal/marketing page; [10-K] = SEC filing; [peer-reviewed] = journal or registered trial; [secondary] = law-firm note, trade press, or marketing blog; [anecdote] = forum post.

Note on tool access: Upwork's support page for Boosted Proposals, OpenAI's prepaid-billing help article, and the EUR-Lex text of Regulation 2019/1150 all returned HTTP 403 / empty to the fetch tool, so those are cited via search-engine summaries of the primary page or via law-firm summaries. Flagged inline where it matters.

## Key question 1: Prepaid credit models at API/usage platforms and marketplaces — why prepaid, stated reasons, bundle discounts

### Takeaway
Across OpenAI, Anthropic, Twilio, MTurk, Prolific and Upwork, the stated reason for prepaid balances is budgeting/predictability and (for Twilio explicitly) credit risk; none of the API platforms publicly offers a volume discount on credit bundles — prepaid credits are sold 1:1 to the invoice price, expire after about a year, and are non-refundable. Bundle discounts appear only where the "credit" is a proprietary unit (Upwork Connects via subscription tiers), not where it is a dollar-pegged balance.

### Cited Findings
- OpenAI's official stated reason for prepaid billing is budgeting: it "helps developers know what they are committing to upfront which can provide more predictability for budgeting and spend management"; purchased credits expire after 1 year, are non-refundable unless required by law, minimum purchase is $5, auto-reload is on by default during setup. [vendor, help article summarised by search; direct fetch returned 403] — [OpenAI Help: What is prepaid billing](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8264644-what-is-prepaid-billing)
- A third-party billing vendor's teardown argues the OpenAI model "eliminates the risk of users running up massive bills they can't pay" — that is the vendor's analysis, not an OpenAI statement. [secondary] — [Dodo Payments OpenAI billing deconstruction](https://docs.dodopayments.com/developer-resources/billing-deconstructions/openai)
- Anthropic's Supplemental Credit Terms (effective 4 Mar 2024): "Usage Credits expire one calendar year from the date Anthropic sends the Confirmation Notice"; credits are "non-refundable"; "Customer may not transfer or sell Credits, whether paid for or provided as part of a promotion"; "Credits are not legal tender or currency of any kind and have no cash value"; promotional credits "expire at the time indicated when issued" or after one year. [vendor, fetched directly] — [Anthropic credit terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/credit-terms)
- Anthropic ties rate-limit tiers to cumulative purchases: Tier 2 requires a cumulative $40 of credits and raises the monthly usage limit to $500; "You're not allowed to deposit more than your monthly spend limit." [vendor] — [Anthropic support: advance to Tier 2](https://support.anthropic.com/en/articles/10366389-how-can-i-advance-my-api-usage-to-tier-2); a third-party table lists Tier 2 at $100 — conflicting secondary figure — [BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/blog/posts/api-credits-explained)
- Twilio runs two models: pay-as-you-go prepaid balance with auto-recharge, and monthly invoicing "provided for high-volume customers with a committed annual spend of at least $12,000 USD", paid by ACH/wire after a credit check that can request "a legal company name, a DUNS number, and financial statements showing cash assets". Auto-recharge is recommended because it "helps prevent your project from being suspended for zero or negative balance". [vendor] — [Twilio: billing by invoice](https://support.twilio.com/hc/en-us/articles/223135647-Can-I-set-up-billing-by-invoice-for-my-Twilio-project-); [Twilio: recharge trigger](https://support.twilio.com/hc/en-us/articles/223135607-How-do-I-set-a-recharge-trigger-); [Twilio: payment types](https://support.twilio.com/hc/en-us/articles/49507358452635-About-Payment-Types)
- Amazon Mechanical Turk ran a "Prepaid HITs" balance for requesters (API `GetAccountBalance` "retrieves the Prepaid HITs balance... if you are a Prepaid Requester"); approvals fail if the balance cannot cover reward plus fee; requesters could alternatively opt into AWS Billing, "moving from a pre-payment to a post-payment model". [vendor] — [AWS MTurk API GetAccountBalance](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSMechTurk/latest/AWSMturkAPI/ApiReference_GetAccountBalanceOperation.html); [CloudResearch on AWS Billing](https://www.cloudresearch.com/?p=1809)
- MTurk fee structure (2015 onward): 20% on reward and bonus, plus an additional 20% on HITs with 10 or more assignments (about 40% total). [vendor, confirmed by Engadget 2015] — [MTurk pricing](https://www.mturk.com/pricing); [Engadget 2015](https://www.engadget.com/2015-06-24-amazon-mechanical-turk-price-increase.html)
- The MTurk pricing page, fetched 2026-10-21, now states: "Amazon Mechanical Turk permanently closed on September 30, 2026." [vendor, fetched directly] — [MTurk pricing](https://www.mturk.com/pricing)
- Prolific charges a platform fee "calculated as a percentage on top of participant rewards": 42.8% for corporate customers, 33.3% for academia/non-profits; researchers add funds to a workspace balance by card or bank transfer (bank transfers "may take up to 7 days"), receive an invoice per top-up, and launch studies against the balance. [vendor] — [Prolific pricing](https://researcher-help.prolific.com/en/articles/445239-what-is-your-pricing); [Prolific invoices](https://researcher-help.prolific.com/en/articles/445247-invoices); [Prolific bank wire](https://researcher-help.prolific.com/en/articles/445243-how-to-add-money-via-bank-wire-transfer)
- CloudResearch Connect (Prolific competitor): 3% fee on card top-ups from 18 May 2026, ACH free, 4 business days to credit. [vendor] — [CloudResearch Connect funding](https://connect-researcher-help.cloudresearch.com/hc/en-us/articles/21117760032276)
- User Interviews requires a minimum $2,500 prepaid balance for teams, used for "participant incentives or researcher cancellation fees". [vendor] — [User Interviews prepaid balances](https://userinterviews.com/support/prepaid-balances)
- Upwork (2026 pricing page): Freelancer Basic is free with 10 Connects/month, extra Connects $0.15 each; Freelancer Plus is $19.99/month with more Connects and 0% freelancer service fee; clients pay 3% or 5% (Basic) or 8–10% (Business Plus) marketplace fees when they hire. [vendor] — [Upwork: Is Upwork free?](https://www.upwork.com/resources/is-upwork-free)
- AWS Activate credits: Founders tier "Start with $1,000 USD in Activate Credits", up to $5,000; Portfolio up to $200,000 (requires an Activate Provider Org ID from an accelerator/VC); AI tier $200,000+ invite-only; purpose "Move from prototype to production faster". The page states no expiry; secondary sources say 12–24 months from account activation, with billing reverting to the card at expiry. [vendor + secondary] — [AWS startup credits](https://aws.amazon.com/startups/credits); [Causo comparison 2026](https://hub.causo.ai/startup-cloud-credits-compared-2026)
- Google for Startups cloud credits: comparison blog reports credits "typically last one year" and up to $350,000 for AI-first startups depending on tier. [secondary, unverified against Google's page] — [Causo comparison 2026](https://hub.causo.ai/startup-cloud-credits-compared-2026)

### Inferences
- The API platforms' prepaid balances are stored-value wallets pegged 1:1 to invoice currency, not a separate unit: no bundle discounts, no breakage play beyond the 12-month expiry, and explicit "not currency / no cash value / non-transferable" terms to stay outside e-money regulation and to block secondary markets.
- The real economic functions, reading across OpenAI/Twilio/MTurk/Prolific: (a) credit-risk elimination for small, unverified accounts (Twilio reserves postpaid for credit-checked $12k+ customers; MTurk/Prolific will not launch work the balance cannot cover); (b) a gating ladder that unlocks higher limits as cumulative spend proves the account (Anthropic tiers); (c) budgeting predictability for buyers. Fraud/chargeback reduction is implied by the structure but not stated by any of these vendors.
- Bundle discounts are effective as a pricing lever only where the unit is proprietary and the platform sets the price (Upwork sells Connects at a lower effective price inside a subscription). Where the unit is 1:1 money, platforms avoid discounts because a discount on prepaid money is just a price cut with an arbitrage surface.
- Transfer to A2H: an agent spending under a monthly budget and per-request cap already has the "predictability" benefit from the budget itself; the remaining case for a prepaid balance is credit risk on holds (hold = bid via manual-capture PaymentIntents already covers this per request, but a prepaid balance avoids one authorisation per request and the 7-day auth-expiry problem). Prolific is the closest analogue (business pays into a balance, platform fee is a percentage on top of the participant reward, participants paid out separately).

### Gaps
- No API platform publishes a reason citing fraud or chargebacks for prepaid billing; the risk rationale is only from third parties.
- No evidence found that any usage-based API platform offers discounted credit bundles; could not verify Google for Startups terms from Google's own page.
- Could not fetch OpenAI's prepaid-billing article directly (403); figures come from the search engine's rendering of the page.

## Key question 2: Bonus/discount credit bundles and mental accounting — do buyers spend prepaid credits more freely than cash, and does it survive with B2B buyers and agents?

### Takeaway
The behavioural literature (Prelec & Loewenstein 1998 coupling, Raghubir & Srivastava 2009 denomination effect, gift-card overspend surveys, and a 2026 RCT on 1:1 virtual currencies) consistently finds that decoupling payment from consumption and reframing money as a token raises willingness to spend — but every effect was measured on individual consumers; nothing was found testing it on B2B procurement or on LLM agents, and the agent benchmark literature suggests agents under a stated budget tend to under-spend or mis-track budget rather than respond to currency framing.

### Cited Findings
- Prelec & Loewenstein's 1998 "double-entry" mental accounting model: payment and consumption are two linked accounts whose "coupling" determines pain of paying; cash creates tight coupling, credit cards and prepayment decouple; the model predicts "debt aversion" — people prefer to pay before consuming, and "will tend to prefer flat-rate pay-in-advance pricing schemes to pay-as-you-go". [peer-reviewed, via secondary summaries; the original paper was not fetched] — [Jason Collins course notes on payments](https://cfdm.jasoncollins.blog/payments); [Money on the Mind: pain of paying](https://www.moneyonthemind.org/amp/what-is-the-pain-of-paying)
- Prelec & Simester found MBA students willing to pay "almost twice as much" for an item of unknown value when paying by credit card vs cash. [peer-reviewed, via secondary] — [Psychology Today 2012](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/the-edge-choice/201207/the-way-we-spend-impacts-how-we-spend)
- A later review argues "payment transparency is probably the primary reason for the difference in pain among payment methods" rather than coupling per se. [peer-reviewed review] — [PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11444724/)
- Denomination effect (Raghubir & Srivastava, J. Consumer Research 2009): people are less likely to spend a sum held as one large bill than as many small units; in one study 63% of students given four quarters bought candy vs 26% given a $1 bill; large denominations are "psychologically less fungible" and people deliberately choose them for self-control. [peer-reviewed] — [IDEAS/RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/v36y2009i4p701-713.html); [NPR 2009](https://www.npr.org/2009/05/12/104063298/why-we-spend-coins-faster-than-bills)
- Toccafondi, Di Paolo & Di Guida, Experimental Economics 2026: RCT with 753 UK participants; willingness to pay for loot boxes "was significantly higher when transactions used virtual currencies with a 1:1 exchange rate compared to British pounds"; money-illusion distortions appeared regardless of exchange-rate intuitiveness; non-intuitive rates did not add further distortion. [peer-reviewed, abstract only] — [IMT Lucca repository](https://iris.imtlucca.it/handle/20.500.11771/42198); [Univ. Parma repository](https://air.unipr.it/handle/11381/3056576)
- Conflicting evidence: an Aalto laboratory study of free-to-play games found "the results don't support the notion that the currency conversions would lead to increased spending" and that players process real money and in-game currency differently. [thesis] — [Aalto: Economic decision-making in free-to-play games](https://research.aalto.fi/fi/publications/economic-decision-making-in-free-to-play-games-a-laboratory-exper/)
- Taiwan mobile-game experiments: players prefer cash when virtual-currency top-ups carry extra cost; cash discounts pull players to cash even when they hold in-game currency; holding leftover virtual currency increases willingness to use it. [thesis] — [NTU repository](https://tdr.lib.ntu.edu.tw/jspui/handle/123456789/94560)
- Gift-card overspend: First Data 2018 study reports consumers spent on average $59 above card value (up $21 on the prior year), supermarket cards 94% above value; First Data 2017: $38 average, 75% overspent. UK GCVA 2024: 68% of redeemers spent more than the card value, average £10 over a £30 card. All are industry-sponsored self-report surveys. [secondary/industry] — [PaymentsJournal on First Data 2018](https://www.paymentsjournal.com/first-data-prepaid-consumer-insights-study/amp/); [Digital Transactions on GCVA 2024](https://www.digitaltransactions.net/with-gift-cards-consumers-are-spending-much-more-than-the-gift-amount/)
- Gift-card breakage: Starbucks FY2024 10-K reports $187.6M (company-operated) plus $20.0M (licensed) breakage revenue, i.e. about $207.6M; stored-value/loyalty liability of $1,718.7M at 29 Sep 2024; "the majority of stored value cards are redeemed within one year"; US cards have no expiry and no fees. [10-K] — [Starbucks FY2024 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/829224/000082922424000057/sbux-20240929.htm)
- Aggregate US unredeemed gift-card value is reported variously as $23B (Sun-Times 2023, $187/person) or $15B (Worldmetrics) — conflicting secondary figures. [secondary] — [Chicago Sun-Times 2023](https://chicago.suntimes.com/business/2023/12/26/24015245/secret-life-gift-cards-heres-what-happens-billions-unspent-retail-restaurants-each-year); [Worldmetrics](https://worldmetrics.org/gift-cards-industry-statistics/)
- LLM-agent budget behaviour (2025–2026 benchmarks, arXiv, not peer-reviewed): BAGEN finds frontier agents keep spending on tasks unlikely to succeed and are "overly optimistic about remaining budget" (budget-awareness correlates only ~0.35 with task skill); ComboShoppingBench finds agents told a budget is "around N" under-spend far more often than over-spend (49% in range); EcoAgent-Bench finds giving agents more budget does not improve choices; EconEvals (Mar 2025) finds reasoning models better at building within-budget procurement plans; E-Commerce Bench finds every tested model sent part of its procurement budget to fraudulent suppliers. [preprints] — [BAGEN](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.00198); [ComboShoppingBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.09282); [EcoAgent-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.05519); [EconEvals](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.18825); [E-Commerce Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.30730)

### Inferences
- Three distinct mechanisms get conflated under "credits make people spend more": (1) prepayment decoupling (sunk cost feels free at use time); (2) token reframing / money illusion (1:1 virtual currency raises WTP even with a trivial exchange rate — the 2026 RCT is the cleanest evidence); (3) windfall/gift framing (gift-card overspend). Only (1) applies to a dollar-pegged prepaid wallet; (2) requires a renamed unit; (3) requires the credits to be bonus/free.
- The evidence is entirely consumer-side, small-stakes (loot boxes, candy, gift cards). Nothing found tests whether a corporate buyer, procurement officer, or an agent under a numeric budget spends a "credit" balance more loosely than a euro balance. The agent benchmarks point the other way: agents treat a stated budget as a hard number and tend to under-spend or mis-track it, with no evidence they are affected by the label on the unit.
- For A2H specifically: an agent bidding EUR 2–60 per answer under a monthly budget and per-request cap is the worst audience for mental-accounting effects — the spending decision is made by code against a numeric cap, not by a person feeling pain of paying. Any "spend more freely" uplift from credits would have to act on the human principal when they set the budget, not on the agent at bid time. The 2026 RCT's "1:1 currency still inflates WTP" result is the one finding that might transfer to the human setting the budget, but it is untested in B2B.
- Breakage is real money at consumer scale (Starbucks ~$208M/yr on a ~$1.7B float) but requires non-expiring consumer balances at volume; a B2B balance with auto-reload and 12-month expiry (OpenAI/Anthropic model) generates little breakage and would be a trust cost in the EU.

### Gaps
- No study found on mental accounting / coupling in B2B procurement or on corporate prepaid balances.
- No study found on whether LLM agents' spending responds to currency framing or bundle discounts; the benchmarks test budget adherence only.
- The original Prelec & Loewenstein (1998) and Toccafondi et al. (2026) full texts were not retrieved; effect sizes for the 2026 RCT are unknown.

## Key question 3: Signup credits and referral credits — documented results, fraud, cost per acquired user, guardrails

### Takeaway
The canonical referral cases (Dropbox: 100k to 4M users in 15 months with storage not cash; PayPal: $20/$20 then cut to $10 and $5, roughly $60M total) are well known but only through secondary marketing sources with no measured cost per acquired user; the documented failure is self-referral and low-commitment exploitation (Uber's $50k promo-code case), and the guardrails that platforms published are: pay only on a verified qualifying action, delay payout past the refund window, cap rewards, link devices/IPs/payment instruments, and reserve clawback.

### Cited Findings
- Dropbox referral: 500MB extra storage to both referrer and referee, capped at 16GB; growth from 100,000 to 4 million users in 15 months (3900%). Some blogs misdescribe the 3900% as a conversion-rate change. [secondary, no primary source] — [Viral Loops on Dropbox](https://viral-loops.com/blog/dropbox-grew-3900-simple-referral-program/?authuser=0); [GrowSurf](https://growsurf.com/blog/dropbox-referral-program/)
- PayPal referral: $20 for opening an account plus $20 per referral, later reduced to $10 and then $5; total incentive spend "roughly $60m" attributed to Elon Musk; 7–10% daily growth. No cost-per-acquired-user figure is published. [secondary] — [Viral Loops on PayPal](https://viral-loops.com/blog/paypal-referral-program-case-study/)
- Uber 2015: a student changed his promo code to a memorable phrase, shared it widely, earned about $50,000 in credits; Uber cancelled them as obtained "via systems and practices that were not allowed by UBER". [secondary] — [Buyapowa on referral fraud](https://www.buyapowa.com/blog/referral-programs-fraud-gaming.md)
- Fraud patterns catalogued by referral-software vendors: self-referral via multiple emails/devices (rated the most common); duplicate-account cycling (create, claim, delete, repeat); return abuse (buy, claim, refund); low-commitment exploitation ("if a program offers a $100 reward for a savings account with a $1 minimum deposit... lots of accounts funded with exactly US$1"); insider abuse. Defenses: match addresses/IPs/cookies/devices, reward only verified purchases, delay rewards past return windows, exclude employee domains. [vendor] — [ReferralCandy: 7 fraud patterns](https://www.referralcandy.com/blog/7-referral-program-fraud-patterns-and-how-to-block-each-one); [Viral Loops on fraud](https://viral-loops.com/blog/fraud-in-referral-programs/)
- Coinbase referral terms: bonus paid only after the invitee completes a qualifying purchase within a set window; only brand-new accounts via the referrer's link; Advanced/Prime purchases excluded. Coinbase One referral: codes deactivated and referrals ineligible if the referrer buys paid ads on Coinbase keywords; program may be changed or ended at any time. [vendor] — [Coinbase referral program](https://help.coinbase.com/coinbase/getting-started/getting-started-with-coinbase/new-customer-incentive/simple-referral); [Coinbase One referral](https://help.coinbase.com/en/coinbase/other-topics/coinbase-one/referral-program)
- Coinbase Card rewards terms (third-party hosted copy) allow recovery of rewards "by deducting their value from the funds in a user's Coinbase account" on a sole-discretion finding of "manufactured spend or fraud or abuse". [secondary copy of vendor terms] — [Cardless-hosted Coinbase rewards terms](https://legal.cardless.com/rewards_terms/coinbase/rewards_terms_and_conditions.pdf)
- Ride-hail promo/driver-bonus fraud at scale: a DOJ case (reported 2021) on a Brazilian gang that created driver accounts with stolen IDs and sold GPS-spoofing tools, with the FBI tracking "more than 2,000 accounts"; a New York case where two defendants sold modified phones to 800 Uber drivers. A vendor explainer describes the fake-rider loop (fake rider + fraudulent driver + stolen card + spoofed trips to earn the driver bonus). [court reporting + vendor] — [The Record](https://therecord.media/brazilian-gang-defrauds-uber-lyft-doordash-using-gps-spoofing-and-stolen-ids); [amNY on Screwber](https://amny.com/transit/feds-charge-drivers-uber-fraud-scheme-screwber); [DataVisor GPS spoofing](https://www.datavisor.com/wiki/gps-spoofing)
- Anthropic's promotional credits carry the same non-transferable / no-cash-value / expiry terms as paid credits, and account closure forfeits them. [vendor] — [Anthropic credit terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/credit-terms)
- AWS Activate's larger tiers are gated behind an accelerator/VC "Org ID" rather than open signup, and all tiers require a paid-tier AWS account. [vendor] — [AWS startup credits](https://aws.amazon.com/startups/credits)

### Inferences
- The two durable guardrails visible across Uber, Coinbase and AWS are (a) tie the reward to a costly, verifiable action by the referee (qualifying purchase, paid-tier account, investor attestation) and (b) make the reward non-cashable and clawback-able. Signup credits that can be converted to cash via the supply side (see Q6) are the dangerous case.
- The widely quoted referral numbers (Dropbox 3900%, PayPal $60M) are pre-2010 and come from marketing blogs; they should not be used as evidence of what a 2026 B2B credit programme would return. No B2B SaaS credit-programme results with numbers were found.
- Transfer to A2H: a signup credit for a principal's agent is a credit paid to a business account; the fraud surface is a principal creating many agent accounts to farm free answers, or colluding with participants to convert free credits to Friday payouts. Gating signup credits behind a verified business identity (Stripe Customer with a successful SCA charge, or VAT-ID check) and paying referral rewards only after the referee's first captured (not merely held) payment mirrors Coinbase's "qualifying purchase" rule. Referral rewards should be credit-only, non-transferable, and expire.

### Gaps
- No primary-source cost-per-acquired-user figures for Dropbox, PayPal, Uber, Airbnb or Lyft referral programmes were found; Airbnb and Lyft programme results were not found in this pass.
- No Robinhood referral-abuse post-mortem was found.
- No published B2B SaaS credit-programme results with numbers were found.

## Key question 4: Earn-to-spend loops — does letting suppliers spend earnings in-platform increase retention or just reduce cash-out?

### Takeaway
No platform or study was found that isolates the effect of letting suppliers spend earnings in-platform on retention; the only quantified two-sided-user data is Airbnb's (about one in four US hosts were also guests in H1 2022, and about 23% of new hosts since 2019 were guests first), which shows overlap exists but not that an in-platform spend loop caused it.

### Cited Findings
- Airbnb: "In the US, nearly one-in-four Airbnb Hosts were also guests on the platform between January and June 2022"; the typical US host-who-was-also-a-guest earned more than $12,000 in that period. [vendor] — [Airbnb newsroom, 2022](https://news.airbnb.com/hosts-who-travel-on-airbnb-typically-earned-more-than-12000-in-h1-2022/)
- Of new hosts joining since 2019, "a little over 23% first started out as guests on Airbnb"; a compilation reports 22% of hosts say they chose to host after being a guest. [secondary] — [Rental Scale-Up host data](https://www.rentalscaleup.com/airbnb-host-data-who-are-airbnb-hosts-why-are-individual-hosts-more-important-than-professional-ones/); [iPropertyManagement](https://ipropertymanagement.com/research/airbnb-statistics.)
- Upwork's 2026 pricing page describes freelancer and client fees separately and does not describe any mechanism for spending freelancer earnings on Connects or on hiring; no source found that addresses earnings reuse. [vendor] — [Upwork: Is Upwork free?](https://www.upwork.com/resources/is-upwork-free)
- Fiverr platform metrics (Feb 2026 trade press): active buyers down 13.6% YoY to 3.1M, spend per buyer $342; Upwork active clients down 6%, spend per client $5,129 (up 7%). Fiverr's Q4 2021 release said 2018-and-earlier cohorts "retained over 110% of revenue from previous year". None of this separates sellers-who-buy. [secondary + vendor IR] — [Invezz Feb 2026](https://invezz.com/news/2026/02/18/fiverr-stock-why-is-it-losing-the-ai-war-against-upwork/); [Fiverr Q4 2021 via BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220216006257/en)
- Reddit replaced its blockchain Community Points (Oct 2023) with a Contributor Program that "allows users to convert Reddit gold and karma into real currency" — i.e. moved from an earn-to-spend token to an earn-to-cash-out model. [secondary] — [Unchained on Reddit Community Points](https://unchainedcrypto.com/reddit-to-discontinue-community-points-citing-scalability-and-regulatory-issues-sparking-community-backlash/)
- Taiwan mobile-game experiments found that "having remaining virtual currency does not decrease but instead enhances players' willingness to use it for transactions" — a retained-balance effect, measured on consumers. [thesis] — [NTU repository](https://tdr.lib.ntu.edu.tw/jspui/handle/123456789/94560)

### Inferences
- The evidence base for earn-to-spend as a retention lever is essentially absent in the sources found. The Airbnb overlap suggests two-sidedness correlates with high-value users, but Airbnb has no in-platform earn-to-spend currency — hosts are paid out in cash and spend cash as guests — so the overlap does not require a credit loop.
- Under EU rules, retained earned balances that a worker can spend but not withdraw would raise PSD2/e-money and worker-payment-fairness concerns; the Reddit pivot toward cash-out rather than token-spend is suggestive of the regulatory direction.
- Transfer to A2H: A2H's supply side (human experts, members of the public) is paid EUR 2–60 per answer and is unlikely to be a buyer of agent-run requests; the Upwork "freelancer who hires" overlap does not obviously exist. Letting participants keep earnings as spendable credit would mostly reduce Friday cash-out (a working-capital benefit to A2H and a cost to participants) with no evidence of a retention benefit, and would create the credit-farming loop in Q6.

### Gaps
- No evidence found on Upwork freelancers who hire, Fiverr sellers who buy, Stack Overflow reputation economics, Duolingo gems, or Kaggle in a form that answers the retention question.
- No study found isolating in-platform spend of earnings vs cash-out on supplier retention.

## Key question 5: Denominating an auction (reserve, bid, clearing) in credits pegged 1:1 vs a floating unit — pros, cons, examples

### Takeaway
Upwork's Boosted Proposals is the clearest live example of a mini-auction denominated in a proprietary, non-1:1 unit (Connects, ~$0.15 each, with four pay-your-own-bid slots per job and charge-on-interaction rules); the documented cost of a floating unit is persistent transparency complaints and freelancers being "charged without getting hired", while the 2026 loot-box RCT shows a 1:1-pegged unit already captures most of the money-illusion uplift, so there is little bidding-behaviour reason to float the unit.

### Cited Findings
- Upwork Boosted Proposals: "The first four results are reserved for Boosted Proposals. ### Inferences
- Pros of a 1:1 euro-pegged unit for A2H's auction: reserve and bid are legible to the human principal setting the budget and to the participant deciding whether to answer; per-request cap and monthly budget are enforced in the same unit as Stripe holds (hold = bid), so no conversion step between bid, hold and capture; no exchange-rate change can ever devalue outstanding bids or reserves; invoicing and VAT are straightforward.
- Pros of a floating/proprietary unit (Connects-style): lets the platform reprice bidding without touching displayed euro prices; allows subscription bundles with lower per-unit cost; the denomination effect suggests small-unit tokens are bid more freely. Cons: the Upwork record shows this produces sustained perceived-unfairness complaints and opaque charging rules; it would make A2H's "fee on the participant side" less transparent (P2B Art. 5 requires explaining how payment affects ranking if boosting ever influences order); and the 2026 RCT indicates a 1:1 unit already produces the WTP uplift, so the marginal behavioural gain from floating is small.
- Agent transfer: an agent bidding under a per-request cap in euros needs no denomination tricks; a floating unit would force the agent (and the principal's budget logic) to carry an exchange rate and would invite arbitrage between credit purchase price and clearing price (Q6). If A2H wants a "Boost"-style lever, Upwork's design of pay-your-own-bid with charge-on-interaction is the one working example; its failure mode is charging for interactions the bidder cannot see.

## Key question 6: Failure modes — credit farming, sybil attacks when earned credits are cash-convertible, buy/redeem arbitrage, devaluation and trust; what detection and limits worked

### Takeaway
The documented failures cluster into: sybil/self-referral farming wherever a credit can be cashed out (Uber's $50k promo case; airdrop farming as the dominant 2023–2025 sybil pattern), bot/fraud-farm participants on research platforms (Prolific now requires live-selfie plus government-ID verification and runs "50+ automated checks"), and unilateral devaluation/cancellation destroying trust (Reddit's tokens fell 84%); the defences that platforms actually deployed are identity verification tied to payout, linkage detection across device/IP/payment instrument, rewards only on verified costly actions, delayed and clawback-able rewards, and non-transferable / no-cash-value credit terms.

### Cited Findings
- Prolific's methodological pack separates "fraudulent accounts, traditional bots, and AI-powered agents"; AI agents "represent a more sophisticated threat... designed to complete surveys entirely without human involvement"; "before anyone can access a study on Prolific, they have to pass identity verification that includes a live video selfie and a government-issued document check"; the quality system "runs 50+ automated checks". [vendor] — [Prolific: how Prolific protects data quality](https://researcher-help.prolific.com/en/articles/621821-methodological-justification-pack-how-prolific-protects-data-quality); [Prolific: bots and data quality](https://www.prolific.com/resources/bots-and-data-quality-on-crowdsourcing-platforms)
- Independent use of Prolific's authenticity check (2026 arXiv paper, US/UK political-outreach study): in the US, 97.2% of participants rated high authenticity and no bots indicated; in the UK, two users flagged; a separate reCAPTCHA check flagged 15 US and 1 UK participants. [preprint] — [arXiv 2603.27413](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.27413)
- Market Research Society data (via TechRadar): 95% of surveyed qualitative researchers reported participant fraud in the last 12 months. [secondary] — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/bots-deepfakes-and-fake-identities-are-sabotaging-user-research)

[survey + vendor] — [arXiv 1707.05104](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.05104); [Shield Labs](https://shieldlabs.ai/blog/how-to-prevent-sybil-attacks)

- E-Commerce Bench: every tested LLM agent sent part of its procurement budget to fraudulent suppliers. [preprint] — [arXiv 2608.30730](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.30730)

### Inferences
- The structural rule across all cases: a credit that is (a) free or discounted at issue and (b) convertible to cash at redemption creates a closed loop that sybils will farm. The loop closes whenever the buyer side and the supply side can be the same actor. For A2H, a principal who controls both an agent account (holding signup/bonus credits) and a participant account (receiving Friday payouts via Stripe Connect) can convert free credits to cash by posting requests and answering them. Discounted bundles (buy EUR 100 of credit for EUR 90) create the same arbitrage at a 10% margin, net of the participant-side fee.
- Defences that transfer directly: (1) issue bonus credits only to a business account with a verified payment method and (ideally) VAT ID; (2) never allow bonus credits to fund a request whose answerer shares device/IP/bank account/Stripe Connect identity with the principal; (3) pay referral/bonus only after the referee's first captured payment from their own card, not from credit; (4) keep bonus credit non-transferable, non-withdrawable, expiring (Anthropic model); (5) withhold the participant-side payout for answers funded by bonus credit until a review window passes (Upwork/MTurk-style discretionary delay); (6) treat sybil detection as probabilistic with manual review, since rule-based clustering produces unacceptable false positives per the MDPI review.
- Prolific's trajectory (selfie + government-ID verification before any study access) is the realistic end state for a platform paying members of the public small sums; A2H's Stripe Connect onboarding (KYC on the payout side) already provides most of this on the supply side.
- Devaluation risk is specific to floating units; a 1:1 euro credit cannot be devalued except by changing expiry or fees. The Reddit case shows that even a non-financial token becomes a trust liability when the platform unilaterally changes its terms.

## Key question 7: Platform fee placement — fee as a spread on credit purchase vs explicit seller-side commission; transparency expectations and EU P2B Regulation 2019/1150

### Takeaway
Practitioner guidance (Gurley 2013; Stripe Connect docs) and the marketplaces studied (MTurk 20–40% on top of reward, Prolific 33.3–42.8% on top of reward, Upwork split between client fee and freelancer fee) point to an explicit percentage disclosed to both sides rather than a spread hidden inside a credit price; EU P2B (applicable since 12 July 2020) requires plain-language terms, disclosure of main ranking parameters and of any paid influence on ranking, and is enforced (AGCOM v Refurbed 2025, France v Booking 2026) — but it governs transparency of terms toward business users, not which side pays the fee.

### Cited Findings
- Gurley, "A Rake Too Far" (Apr 2013): "The most dangerous strategy for any platform company is to price too high"; the rake "becomes part of the landed price for the consumer"; "High rakes also create a natural impetus for suppliers to look elsewhere, which endangers sustainability"; "There is a big difference between what you can extract versus what you should extract." Examples: eBay ~10% (2011), Amazon Marketplace 6–15% (~12% avg), Booking.com 10% agency model vs merchant model >30%, oDesk cut from 30% to 10%, Shutterstock ~70%, Groupon ~38%, Apple/Facebook 30%. Gurley notes his rake estimates "may be inaccurate because some companies make them hard to determine". [primary essay, fetched directly] — [Above the Crowd](https://abovethecrowd.com/2013/04/18/a-rake-too-far/)
- Stripe Connect: "Your platform collects per-payment application fees from connected accounts"; marketplaces "might absorb Stripe fees themselves, but charge a commission when paying out funds"; "Since you handle Stripe fees, the application fee is the only fee that your connected accounts see"; `application_fee_amount` must be positive and less than the charge and is "capped at the captured amount of the charge". No Stripe guidance found on buyer-side surcharges vs seller-side fees. [vendor] — [Stripe: application fees](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/application-fees); [Stripe: SaaS platforms and marketplaces](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/saas-platforms-and-marketplaces); [Stripe: app fees task](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/marketplace/tasks/app-fees)
- MTurk: fee is an explicit 20% (40% for 10+ assignments) on top of the worker reward, paid by the requester. [vendor] — [MTurk pricing](https://www.mturk.com/pricing)
- Prolific: platform fee is "calculated as a percentage on top of participant rewards" (42.8% corporate / 33.3% academic), paid by the researcher; invoices are issued per top-up. [vendor] — [Prolific pricing](https://researcher-help.prolific.com/en/articles/445239-what-is-your-pricing)

- P2B Regulation 2019/1150: Article 5 requires online intermediation services to "set out in their terms and conditions the main parameters determining ranking and their underlying rationale"; where a business can pay to influence ranking, the terms must describe how that payment can affect results; platforms must describe differentiated treatment of their own goods; algorithms need not be revealed where disclosure would enable manipulation. - P2B Article 3(1): terms must be in plain and intelligible language, easily available, set out grounds for suspension/termination, include information on "additional distribution channels and potential affiliate programmes" through which the platform may market the business user's offers, and describe the effects of the terms on IP. [partial excerpt of the Regulation text] — [Lexpera excerpt of OJ L 186/57](https://www.lexpera.com.tr/literatur/kitaplar/ek-1-p2-tuzugu-978-625-432-278-5)
- P2B enforcement: AGCOM found Refurbed Marketplace in breach of Art. 5(1) in November 2025; France's regulator pressed Booking to amend its terms in early 2026. [secondary, law-firm] — [BDK Advokati](https://bdkadvokati.com/p2b-regulation-has-teeth-french-and-italian-regulators-show)
- Anthropic/OpenAI sell credits at face value with no spread; the "fee" is in the per-token price list, not in the credit purchase. [vendor] — [Anthropic credit terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/credit-terms); [OpenAI prepaid billing](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8264644-what-is-prepaid-billing)

### Inferences
- Among the comparable platforms, none hides the take rate inside the credit price; the labour/research marketplaces (MTurk, Prolific) charge an explicit percentage on top of the worker reward on the buyer side, which makes the reward figure the participant sees identical to the gross the buyer sees minus a stated fee. A spread on credit purchase (e.g. EUR 1 buys 0.85 credit) is mathematically a buyer-side fee but is less legible to both sides, breaks the 1:1 peg that Q5 argued for, and would have to be disclosed in P2B-compliant plain language anyway for business users on the supply side (if any A2H participants are businesses).
- P2B applies to A2H to the extent its participants are "business users" (experts acting in a trade/profession) offering to consumers in the EU; members of the public answering as private individuals are outside P2B's business-user definition, but A2H cannot easily know which. The safe reading is: publish the fee, the reserve/bid mechanics and any paid-boost effect on ordering in plain terms, and give 15 days' notice of changes (standard P2B obligation per the law-firm summaries).
- Gurley's argument is about level, not placement: a high take on EUR 2–60 answers lands in the participant's net and in the principal's effective price either way; the placement decision is about who sees the number. A participant-side fee (current plan) has the Upwork-style risk of participants perceiving the platform as taking from their earnings; a buyer-side fee on top (Prolific/MTurk model) keeps the participant's reward whole and lets the principal's agent treat fee-inclusive cost as the budget line. Stripe's mechanics (application_fee capped at captured amount) support either.
- Agent transfer: an agent's per-request cap is most naturally expressed as fee-inclusive gross (what Stripe captures). If the fee is on the participant side, the agent's bid equals the hold equals the capture and the fee is netted at Connect payout; if the fee is buyer-side, bid plus fee must fit the cap. Either way an explicit percentage is machine-legible; a credit-price spread is not.
